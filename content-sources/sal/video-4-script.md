# Video 4: 12 Fast Food Chains I Wouldn't Feed My Grandkids (And 4 I Would)

**Thumbnail tekst:** NOT MY GRANDKIDS
**Dužina:** ~15 min (oko 2.563 izgovorenih reči; Sal priča energično, oko 170 reči u minuti)
**Oznake:** [SAL] = Sal priča u kameru, [B-ROLL] = slika ili stock snimak preko glasa, [TEKST] = tekst na ekranu

**Napomena:** prvi video koji imenuje prave lance. Svaka činjenica o lancu je iz izvora u sekciji "IZVORI" na dnu (zvanični sajt lanca, saopštenje lanca ili veliki medij). Sve ostalo Sal govori kao lični ukus ("I'd rather", "for me", "my style"). Nema zdravstvenih tvrdnji, nema reči "unhealthy", "chemicals" i slično. Svaki lanac dobija bar jedan iskren kompliment. Rang je Salovo mišljenje po njegovim kriterijumima (sveže ili smrznuto, šta se stvarno kuva u restoranu, sastojci po zvaničnim izjavama, vrednost za novac), ne ocena kvaliteta ili bezbednosti. B-ROLL: samo generička hrana, bez logoa, ambalaže sa brendom i natpisa lanaca.

**Stil:** kratke, udarne rečenice, pa pauza ("..."). Odbrojavanje od #12 (najmanje po Salovom ukusu) do #1, pa nagrada: 4 lanca u koja vodi unuke. [TEKST] sa brojem i imenom lanca na početku svakog segmenta.

## HOOK (0:00–0:52)

[SAL]
My granddaughter is seven. Last month, in the car, she says, "Nonno, can we get fast food?"

And I froze. Not because I'm a snob. I'm not. I washed dishes in a chain restaurant at sixteen. I love a drive-thru.

I froze because I know what's behind the counter.

[B-ROLL: a kid's hand reaching for a french fry in a paper bag, car interior]

I'm Sal Romano. Twenty-five years running Michelin-starred kitchens. New York. Milan. Then twelve years in my own place in New Jersey, Romano's, with my wife Angela.

So today, I'm doing something I've never done on this channel. I'm naming names.

[TEKST: 16 CHAINS. 12 NO. 4 YES.]

Sixteen of the biggest fast food chains in America. Twelve I wouldn't take my grandkids to. And four I would. Happily. With my own wallet.

And listen. This is not a hit job. Every one of these places does something right, and I'll tell you what. But some of them do a lot more cooking than others.

Let's go.

## HOW SAL JUDGES (0:52–1:19)

[SAL]
Four questions. That's my whole system.

[TEKST: 1. FRESH OR FROZEN? 2. COOKED HERE OR REHEATED? 3. WHAT DO THEY SAY IS IN IT? 4. WORTH THE MONEY?]

One. Fresh or frozen? Two. Is it actually cooked in that building, or just finished there? Three. What's in it, according to the company itself. Not rumors. Their own words. And four. Is it worth the money?

That's it. No diets, no doctors. I'm a cook, not a nutritionist. I'm judging the cooking.

Number twelve to number one. Worst to least bad. Then the four good ones. Your reward for staying.

## #12: JACK IN THE BOX (1:19–2:00)

[SAL]
Number twelve. Jack in the Box.

[TEKST: #12 JACK IN THE BOX]

I'll start with the compliment. Most of their restaurants are open twenty-four hours, and breakfast runs all night. When I was a young line cook, finishing at two in the morning? A place like that was a church.

But the tacos.

[B-ROLL: a crispy folded taco being dropped into a fryer basket]

Fox News looked into this. The tacos are made in factories. Tortillas filled with the beef mixture, shrink-wrapped, frozen, and shipped to the stores. Then they're fried, topped, and served.

Born in a factory. Finished in a fryer.

And people love them! Hundreds of millions a year. I understand. Crunchy, salty, two a.m. I get it.

But for my granddaughter? That's not cooking. That's reheating. With extra steps.

---

## #11: BURGER KING (2:00–2:45)

[SAL]
Number eleven. Burger King.

[TEKST: #11 BURGER KING]

Compliment first. The flame. Real flame-broiling gives you a real flavor. That smoky edge? I respect it. And in 2020, they took artificial colors, flavors and preservatives out of the Whopper. CNBC reported it. Good move.

[B-ROLL: burger patties moving over open flames on a broiler]

So why number eleven?

Here's my rule from forty years. When a place uses fresh beef, believe me... they tell you. It's on the sign. It's in every commercial. I looked. I couldn't find Burger King saying their patties are fresh.

And for me, a burger lives or dies on the beef. At Romano's, my staff burger was ground that morning. Chuck, a little short rib. Salt. That's it. Nothing to hide behind.

So a nice flame... but I want to know what's on the grill. Capisce?

---

## #10: TACO BELL (2:45–3:30)

[SAL]
Number ten. Taco Bell.

[TEKST: #10 TACO BELL]

Now, this one is about the beef. And I'm using their own words.

Back in 2011, Taco Bell ran ads explaining their seasoned beef. Eighty-eight percent beef. The other twelve percent? Their recipe. Water, spices, oats, caramelized sugar, yeast, and a few more things.

[TEKST: 88% BEEF / 12% "RECIPE" (Taco Bell, 2011)]

Good for them for saying it out loud. Honest.

[B-ROLL: seasoned ground beef in a steel pan, tortillas warming]

But when I make taco meat for the grandkids? Beef. Onion. Garlic. Cumin, chili, salt. Done. My grandson eats three, and I don't need a list.

Now the compliment, and it's a big one. Value. In 2024 they launched a value menu with ten items for three dollars or less. In this economy? That's a gift. And they have real vegetarian options. Beans, potatoes. Respect.

Cheap and cheerful. Just not my taco.

---

## #9: PIZZA HUT (3:30–4:19)

[SAL]
Number nine. Pizza Hut.

[TEKST: #9 PIZZA HUT]

Listen. Pizza Hut is a memory for a lot of people. Red cups, the pan pizza, birthday parties. My own kids had a birthday party in one. Back in the eighties.

[B-ROLL: a thick, golden pan pizza being lifted from a dark round pan]

And credit where it's due. In 2019, they rebuilt the pan pizza for the first time in about forty years. New pan, new cheese blend, new sauce, crispier crust. Three years of work. That's a company that cares about its crust.

So why number nine? Personal taste.

I'm from Paterson, New Jersey. My grandfather was from Calabria. For me, pizza is thin, blistered, and it comes out of a very hot oven while you watch. A thick crust soaked in oil in a pan? Delicious.

But it's a different food. Teaching my grandkids pizza? I start at home. With dough I made that morning.

---

## #8: SUBWAY (4:19–5:13)

[SAL]
Number eight. Subway.

[TEKST: #8 SUBWAY]

Compliment. Subway bakes bread in its restaurants every day. They've said it for over thirty years. That's why the whole store smells like a bakery. Smart.

[B-ROLL: rows of sandwich rolls baking in a small commercial oven]

But the dough is not made there. Food industry reporting says it comes from outside bakeries, frozen. Then it's baked in the store.

Baked here. Born somewhere else.

And my favorite food story of the decade. In 2020, Ireland's Supreme Court ruled on a tax case. Their bread had sugar at about ten percent of the flour weight. Irish tax law allows two percent for "bread." So for tax purposes? The court said it's not bread.

[TEKST: IRELAND, 2020: "NOT BREAD" (for tax purposes)]

Subway said: "Subway's bread is, of course, bread."

Angela's focaccia had flour, water, yeast, salt, olive oil. A pinch of sugar for the yeast. That's bread. For me, sandwich bread shouldn't taste like a dinner roll's dessert.

Fresh vegetables you can see, though? That I like. Number eight.

---

## #7: McDONALD'S (5:13–6:06)

[SAL]
Number seven. The big one. McDonald's.

[TEKST: #7 McDONALD'S]

Compliment first, and I mean it. In 2018 they switched the Quarter Pounder to fresh beef, cooked when you order. The AP reported it. For a company that size? That's a big, expensive decision. Bravo.

But, same article. The Big Mac and the other burgers? Still frozen beef.

[B-ROLL: a burger patty searing on a flat-top grill]

And the fries. I love those fries. Everybody loves those fries. But McDonald's tells you on their own website how they're made. The suppliers peel, cut and blanch the potatoes. Then dry them, partially fry them, and freeze them. The restaurant finishes them in the fryer.

[TEKST: PEEL → CUT → BLANCH → PARTLY FRY → FREEZE → FRY AGAIN]

Is that crazy? No. It's a smart system. That's why they taste the same in Ohio and in Oregon.

But that's a factory fry. Finished in a restaurant.

Now here's what Sal orders. If I'm there, it's the Quarter Pounder. The fresh one. Tell the grandkids the secret.

Quick one before we keep going. Everything I check before I eat anywhere, I put on one page. What to order, what to skip, what to ask. "Sal's 25 Rules for Eating Out." It's free. First link in the description. Put it on the fridge.

## #6: DOMINO'S (6:28–7:12)

[SAL]
Number six. Domino's.

[TEKST: #6 DOMINO'S]

This one surprised me. Domino's says all of its hand-tossed dough is made fresh and delivered to stores every day. Not frozen. One of their supply centers in Indiana sends out ninety thousand dough balls a day.

[B-ROLL: a hand stretching a fresh ball of pizza dough on a floured counter]

Ninety thousand! Madonna. In Milan I had a pastry cook who complained about making two hundred rolls.

So the dough? Fresh. Respect.

Why only number six? My personal problem with delivery pizza. Any delivery pizza. Hot pizza in a closed box is like a sauna. The steam goes right back into the crust. Twenty minutes in a car, and the crispy part gives up.

That's not their fault. That's physics. Pick it up yourself. Open the lid a crack on the way home. Trust Sal.

---

## #5: ARBY'S (7:12–8:02)

[SAL]
Number five. Arby's.

[TEKST: #5 ARBY'S]

People say wild things about Arby's beef online. Let's go by what's reported.

[B-ROLL: a roast being thinly sliced on a deli slicer]

The roast beef comes in a bag with a basting liquid. The restaurant thaws it, then roasts it for around three hours. Right there in the building. Then they slice it.

So yes. It's real beef. And it's really roasted in the restaurant. That's more cooking than half this list.

Here's why it's not higher. For me, a roast is something you build. You season it. You sear it. You smell it the whole afternoon. At Romano's, Sunday roast beef went in at ten in the morning, and the whole dining room smelled like my nonna's house by noon.

A roast that shows up already seasoned in a bag? It's cooked here. But it was made somewhere else. Thin-sliced, warm, honest sandwich, though. Number five.

---

## #4: KFC (8:02–8:46)

[SAL]
Number four. KFC.

[TEKST: #4 KFC]

Now we're getting closer to real cooking.

KFC says its chicken is hand-breaded and made fresh in the restaurant. They even shared their breading routine with Fox News. Roll it seven times. Toss it ten times. Press it seven times. Seven, ten, seven.

[B-ROLL: hands tossing raw chicken pieces in seasoned flour]

That's technique! That's a station! I had cooks in New York who couldn't follow a three-step recipe.

And then pressure fryers. The Colonel's idea, back in the forties. Crisp outside, juicy inside.

So why not in my top four? The sides. Personal taste. When I make mashed potatoes, I want lumps. I want butter you can see. I want somebody peeling potatoes in the back. The chicken earns its spot. For me, the rest of the bucket doesn't keep up.

---

## #3: WENDY'S (8:46–9:35)

[SAL]
Number three. Wendy's.

[TEKST: #3 WENDY'S]

Oh, I like Wendy's. Fresh, never-frozen beef in the contiguous U.S. and Alaska. Delivered refrigerated, several times a week. Cooked on the grill in the restaurant.

[B-ROLL: square burger patties sizzling on a flat-top]

And the square patty? It hangs over the bun so you can see the beef. That's confidence.

But my favorite part is the chili. Wendy's tells this story themselves. Fresh beef doesn't keep forever. So Dave Thomas asked: what do we do with the leftover hamburger? He turned it into chili. Beans, vegetables, spices.

[TEKST: LEFTOVER BURGERS → CHILI]

That's not a shortcut. That's a grandmother's move! Every good kitchen does this. At Romano's, yesterday's bread was tomorrow's meatballs.

So why is Wendy's still on the "no" list? Honestly? My grandkids don't order the burger. They order nuggets and fries. And for me, that's not where the fresh story lives. Close, though. Very close.

---

## #2: POPEYES (9:35–10:18)

[SAL]
Number two. Popeyes.

[TEKST: #2 POPEYES]

Popeyes says its chicken is marinated for at least twelve hours. Then hand-battered and breaded in buttermilk. And fried fresh.

Twelve hours! That's not fast food. That's a plan.

[B-ROLL: chicken pieces resting in a marinade, then dipped in buttermilk batter]

When I was a line cook, the old chef told me, "Salvatore, the flavor goes in the night before. Not at the fryer." He was right. And they're doing it.

So why not top four? Two reasons. Both personal. One. The spice. My granddaughter is seven. She cried at black pepper last Christmas. For grown-ups, perfect. For her? Too much.

Two. For me, the side dishes don't match the chicken. The chicken is the star. The rest is a supporting actor.

If it's just me and Angela? We're going.

---

## #1 ON THE "NO" LIST: PANDA EXPRESS (10:18–11:10)

[SAL]
And the least bad of the twelve. Number one. Panda Express.

[TEKST: #1 (OF THE "NO" LIST) PANDA EXPRESS]

I almost put them in the good four. Here's why. Panda says its cooks wok-fire the dishes in small batches through the day, in open kitchens. And their people prepare fresh vegetables. Broccoli, peppers, onions. Every day.

[B-ROLL: a cook tossing vegetables in a flaming wok]

Real woks. Real fire. Real knives. I respect that so much.

And the Orange Chicken! Invented in 1987 by their chef Andy Kao. NBC reported they sold over a hundred and fifteen million pounds in one year. A hundred and fifteen million. That's a dish.

So why not top four? Personal taste again. For me, a lot of that menu leans sweet. Sweet sauce, sweet glaze. Grandkids love sweet. That's exactly my problem.

And I like my stir-fry out of the wok and onto my plate. Not waiting in a tray.

Best of the twelve. Just not quite a yes.

## YES #4: CHICK-FIL-A (11:19–11:53)

[SAL]
Number four. Chick-fil-A.

[TEKST: YES #4 CHICK-FIL-A]

Their own words: a whole, boneless chicken breast, hand-breaded daily in the kitchen of every restaurant. Every one.

[B-ROLL: a whole chicken breast pressed into breading by hand]

The lemonade? Three ingredients. Lemon juice, water, sugar. That's how my mother made it.

Biscuits baked fresh every morning at their free-standing restaurants. Produce delivered up to six times a week.

That's a kitchen. Not a warehouse.

The only thing I don't love? The line. Twenty cars, every time. But you know what? A line that long means somebody's doing something right. My granddaughter gets nuggets and a lemonade, and Nonno sleeps fine.

---

## YES #3: CULVER'S (11:53–12:34)

[SAL]
Number three. Culver's.

[TEKST: YES #3 CULVER'S]

Fresh, never-frozen beef since the first restaurant in 1984. Cooked to order. Seared on the flat-top. Top bun buttered and toasted. That came from the founder's mother's recipe.

[B-ROLL: a burger patty pressed onto a hot griddle, a buttered bun toasting]

And the founder, Craig Culver, said something I'd put on my kitchen wall. "Frozen beef loses moisture, and as any chef will tell you, when you lose moisture, you lose flavor."

Craig! Come cook with me!

[TEKST: "When you lose moisture, you lose flavor." Craig Culver]

And then the frozen custard. Made in small batches through the day, with a different flavor of the day since 1984. Angela made gelato at Romano's every morning. Small batches, every day. That's the only way it's right.

The grandkids get custard. I get a butter burger. Everybody wins.

---

## YES #2: FIVE GUYS (12:34–13:19)

[SAL]
Number two. Five Guys.

[TEKST: YES #2 FIVE GUYS]

Never frozen. Hand-pattied beef. The fries? Cut right in the store, every day. They even put up a board telling you which farm the potatoes came from.

[B-ROLL: whole potatoes going through a manual fry cutter, a burlap potato sack in the corner]

Every morning the crew tears the lettuce by hand. Chops the onions. Slices the peppers. That's prep. That's the morning I lived for forty years.

And they fry in peanut oil, twice. That's how you make a real fry.

The bad news? The bill. The first time I took the whole family, I looked at that receipt and sat down. A burger, fries and a drink adds up fast. And they give you so many fries, half the bag ends up in the car.

So share the fries. One bag for two kids. Trust me, it's enough.

## YES #1: IN-N-OUT (13:19–14:08)

[SAL]
And my number one. The place I'd take my grandkids tomorrow. In-N-Out.

[TEKST: YES #1 IN-N-OUT]

Listen to this. Their own words. "We don't even own a microwave or freezer."

No. Freezer. In fast food. Do you know how rare that is?

[B-ROLL: fresh potatoes being cut into fries, hand-leafed lettuce, a burger on a griddle]

They make their own patties from fresh, whole chucks. The fries come from whole potatoes, cut in the store. The lettuce is leafed by hand. Shakes made with real ice cream. The buns use a slow-rising dough.

And the menu fits on a postcard. Burger, cheeseburger, double, fries, shakes. That's it. Short menu, fresh food. That's the oldest rule in my business.

A small complaint? The fries. For me, a fresh-cut fry needs a little more color. So I ask for them well done. Problem solved.

When my granddaughter asks, "Nonno, can we get fast food?"... this is where we go.
