# CHECKLIST CONTENT — Walter's Home Check

Format for each item:
`Item text` | Tip | Priority if marked Problem | Who to call | Modes

Modes: B = Buying a home, A = Yearly home check, W = Get ready for winter

---

## 1. Outside & Grounds (icon: tree)

- Ground slopes away from the house on all sides | Water should run away from your foundation, not toward it. Stand at each corner and look. If the dirt slopes toward the house, that's how basements get wet. | High | Landscaper / grading contractor | B A
- No standing water or soggy spots near the foundation | A puddle that sits next to the house after rain is water looking for a way in. | Medium | Landscaper / drainage contractor | B A
- Gutters are attached, clean and not sagging | Clogged gutters overflow right where you don't want water — against the foundation. | Medium | Gutter contractor | B A W
- Downspouts send water at least 4–6 feet away from the house | A downspout dumping water at the foundation undoes everything the gutters are doing. A cheap extension fixes a lot. | Medium | Gutter contractor | B A W
- Trees and large shrubs are not touching the roof or walls | Branches scrape shingles, drop leaves in gutters and give critters a free bridge to your roof. | Low | Tree service | B A
- Large trees are not too close to the foundation | Big roots near the house can mess with foundations and sewer lines. Note how close the biggest trees are. | Medium | Arborist / sewer contractor | B
- Driveway and walkways have no major cracks or trip hazards | Small cracks are normal. Big heaved slabs are a trip hazard and sometimes a sign of drainage trouble. | Low | Concrete contractor | B A
- Retaining walls are straight, not leaning or bulging | A leaning retaining wall is losing the fight with the dirt behind it. Those get expensive. | High | Structural engineer | B
- Decks and porches feel solid, no soft or rotten boards | Push on posts and step on boards near the house. Soft wood means rot. | High | Deck contractor / carpenter | B A
- Deck is attached to the house with metal hardware, not just nails | Decks that pull away from the house are a real safety issue. Look underneath where the deck meets the wall. | High | Deck contractor | B
- Railings on steps, porches and decks are solid | Grab every railing and give it a good shake. It should not move. | High | Carpenter | B A
- Outside faucets work and don't leak | Turn each one on and off. Check for drips inside the wall behind it too, if you can see it. | Low | Plumber | B A
- Outside faucets shut off / drained for winter | A frozen outdoor faucet can split the pipe inside the wall, and you won't know until spring. Disconnect hoses. | High | Plumber | W

## 2. Roof (icon: house-roof)

- Shingles lie flat — no curling, cupping or missing pieces | Look from the ground with binoculars if you need to. Never climb a roof you're not comfortable on. | High | Roofer | B A W
- No bare spots or heavy granule loss on shingles | Lots of granules in the gutters or at downspout exits means the shingles are wearing out. | Medium | Roofer | B A
- Roof age is known (ask the seller or check records) | Asphalt shingle roofs often last about 20–25 years. Knowing the age tells you what's coming. | Medium | Roofer | B
- Roof line looks straight — no sagging | A dip in the roof line can mean problems with the structure underneath. | High | Structural engineer / roofer | B
- Flashing around chimney, vents and skylights looks sealed | Most roof leaks I'd look for start at flashing, not in the middle of the shingles. | Medium | Roofer | B A W
- Chimney is straight, bricks and mortar in good shape | Loose bricks or crumbling mortar on a chimney let water in and can become a falling hazard. | Medium | Chimney / masonry contractor | B A
- Chimney has a cap | A cap keeps rain, animals and debris out of the flue. | Low | Chimney sweep | B A W
- No moss or heavy debris sitting on the roof | Moss holds water against shingles and shortens their life. | Low | Roofer / roof cleaning | A W

## 3. Outside Walls, Windows & Doors (icon: window)

- Siding has no cracks, holes, gaps or rot | Pay attention to the bottom edge and around windows — that's where rot starts. | Medium | Siding contractor / carpenter | B A
- Paint or caulk is not peeling, cracked or missing | Paint and caulk are what keep water out of the wood. When they go, the wood is next. | Low | Painter | B A
- Brick or stone walls have no large cracks | Stair-step cracks in brick or cracks wider than a coin edge deserve a closer look. | High | Structural engineer / mason | B
- Windows open, close and lock properly | Test a few in every room. Painted-shut windows are a problem in a fire. | Medium | Window contractor | B A
- No fogging or moisture between double-pane glass | Fog between the panes means the seal failed. Not an emergency, but the insulation value is gone. | Low | Window contractor | B
- Window frames and sills are not soft or rotten | Press gently with your thumb or a key on the bottom corners. | Medium | Carpenter | B A
- Doors open, close and latch without sticking | A door that suddenly sticks can be humidity — or the house shifting. Note it. | Low | Carpenter | B
- Weatherstripping around doors and windows is intact | If you can see daylight around a closed door, you're heating the outdoors. | Low | Handyman | A W

## 4. Foundation & Structure (icon: brick)

- No horizontal cracks in foundation walls | Horizontal cracks are the ones I'd worry about most. They can mean soil pushing on the wall. | High | Structural engineer | B A
- Vertical cracks are hairline only (thinner than a credit card) | Thin vertical cracks are common as concrete cures. Wide ones, or ones with one side sticking out, are a different story. | Medium | Structural engineer | B A
- No foundation walls bowing or leaning inward | Look down the length of the wall. It should be straight. | High | Structural engineer | B
- No stair-step cracks in block or brick foundation | Cracks that zig-zag along the mortar joints can mean settling. | High | Structural engineer | B
- Floors inside feel level — no noticeable slopes or bounce | Put a marble or a ball on the floor in a few rooms. A little roll is an old house. A fast roll is a question. | Medium | Structural engineer | B
- No cracks above door frames or windows inside | Diagonal cracks running from the corners of doors and windows can be a sign of movement. | Medium | Structural engineer | B
- No signs of wood damage from insects (tunnels, sawdust, soft wood) | Termite mud tubes on the foundation look like thin dirt tunnels. Don't ignore them. | High | Pest control / termite inspector | B A

## 5. Basement & Crawlspace (icon: stairs-down)

- No water stains, puddles or damp spots on floor or walls | Check corners and where the floor meets the wall. Water lines on boxes or walls tell old stories. | High | Waterproofing contractor | B A W
- No white chalky deposits on walls (efflorescence) | That white powder is minerals left behind by water coming through the wall. | Medium | Waterproofing contractor | B A
- No musty smell or visible mold | Your nose is one of the best inspection tools. Musty means moisture somewhere. | High | Mold remediation / waterproofing | B A
- Sump pump works (if there is one) | Pour a bucket of water in the pit and make sure the pump kicks on and pumps it out. | High | Plumber | B A W
- Sump pump discharge goes well away from the house | No sense pumping the water out just to have it run right back in. | Medium | Plumber | B A
- Crawlspace has a vapor barrier and is dry | Look with a flashlight from the access door. Standing water or bare wet dirt is a problem. | Medium | Crawlspace contractor | B A
- Beams, joists and posts are solid — no rot, cracks or sagging | Poke suspicious wood with a screwdriver. It should not sink in. | High | Structural engineer / carpenter | B
- No signs of rodents (droppings, chewed insulation) | Mice in the basement are rarely only in the basement. | Low | Pest control | B A W
- Radon test done (or planned) | Radon is a gas you can't see or smell, and it's common in basements. Test kits are cheap. Testing is the only way to know. | High | Radon mitigation specialist | B A

## 6. Attic & Insulation (icon: triangle-house)

- Insulation covers the attic floor evenly | Gaps and thin spots mean heat escapes. Compare a few spots with a ruler. | Medium | Insulation contractor | B A W
- No water stains on roof boards or rafters | Dark stains on the underside of the roof deck mean a leak, now or in the past. Look after a rain if you can. | High | Roofer | B A W
- No daylight coming through the roof boards | Turn off your flashlight. Daylight where it shouldn't be is a hole. | High | Roofer | B A
- Attic vents are open and not blocked by insulation | An attic needs to breathe. Blocked vents trap moisture and heat. | Medium | Roofer / insulation contractor | B A W
- Bathroom and kitchen fans vent outside, not into the attic | Fans dumping moist air into the attic can rot the roof from the inside. | Medium | Electrician / roofer | B
- No signs of animals (nests, droppings) | Squirrels, raccoons and birds love attics. They chew wires too. | Medium | Wildlife removal | B A
- No frost or heavy condensation on roof boards in cold weather | Frost in the attic means warm, wet air from the house is getting up there. | Medium | Insulation contractor | W
- Old fluffy gray-brown or vermiculite insulation noted (do not disturb) | Some older loose insulation can contain asbestos. Don't touch it — get it tested. | High | Asbestos testing professional | B

## 7. Electrical (icon: lightning)

- Main panel has a clear label for each breaker | A labeled panel saves a lot of guessing in an emergency. | Low | Electrician | B A
- No scorch marks, burning smell or buzzing at the panel | Don't open the panel cover yourself. Just look and smell. Any of these means call an electrician now. | High | Electrician | B A
- Panel brand noted (ask about Federal Pacific or Zinsco panels) | Some older panel brands have a bad reputation for breakers not tripping. Worth asking an electrician about. | High | Electrician | B
- Electrical service size known (amps) | Older homes may have small service that struggles with modern use. Ask the seller or an electrician. | Medium | Electrician | B
- Outlets near water have GFCI protection (kitchen, baths, garage, outside, laundry) | Those outlets with test and reset buttons protect you from shock near water. Press test, then reset. | High | Electrician | B A
- Outlets are not loose, cracked or warm to the touch | A warm outlet or switch plate is not normal. | High | Electrician | B A
- Three-prong outlets are actually grounded | A cheap plug-in outlet tester tells you in seconds. | Medium | Electrician | B
- No exposed wiring, open junction boxes or extension cords used as permanent wiring | Wires should be in walls or boxes, not hanging loose. | High | Electrician | B A
- No signs of very old wiring (cloth-covered, knob-and-tube) | Very old wiring may not be safe or insurable. Look in the basement and attic. | High | Electrician | B
- Lights work and don't flicker when appliances turn on | Flickering when the fridge or AC kicks on can mean a loose connection. | Medium | Electrician | B A

## 8. Plumbing (icon: water-drop)

- Water pressure is good at faucets and showers | Turn on a sink and a shower at the same time. A weak trickle is worth asking about. | Medium | Plumber | B A
- No leaks under sinks | Open every cabinet with a flashlight. Feel the bottom of the cabinet for soft or warped wood. | Medium | Plumber | B A
- Drains empty quickly, no gurgling | Fill a sink and let it go. Slow or gurgling drains can mean clogs or venting problems. | Medium | Plumber | B A
- Toilets flush well, don't run and don't rock | Sit on it and lean side to side. A rocking toilet can leak at the base and rot the floor. | Medium | Plumber | B A
- Type of water pipes noted (copper, PEX, galvanized, polybutylene) | Some older pipe types, like gray polybutylene or old galvanized steel, have a history of problems. | High | Plumber | B
- Main water shut-off located and it works | Find it before you need it. In a burst-pipe emergency, every minute counts. | Medium | Plumber | B A W
- Exposed pipes in unheated areas are insulated | Pipes in crawlspaces, garages and outside walls are the ones that freeze. | High | Plumber | W
- Sewer line camera inspection considered (older home or big trees) | A sewer line replacement is one of the most expensive surprises. A camera inspection is cheap insurance. | High | Plumber / sewer contractor | B
- Well and septic inspected (if the home has them) | Wells and septic systems need their own inspection. Don't skip it. | High | Well & septic specialist | B

## 9. Water Heater (icon: tank)

- Age of the water heater is known (check the label) | Tank water heaters often last around 8–12 years. The date is usually on the label or in the serial number. | Medium | Plumber | B A
- No rust, corrosion or water around the base | Rust at the bottom of the tank is usually the beginning of the end. | High | Plumber | B A
- Relief valve has a pipe running down toward the floor | That valve and pipe are a safety device. The pipe should point down, not at someone's face. | High | Plumber | B
- Gas water heater vent pipe is connected and sloped up | A loose vent can let exhaust gases into the house. | High | Plumber / HVAC technician | B A
- Water heater is strapped (in earthquake areas) | Many areas require straps so the tank can't tip over. | Low | Plumber | B

## 10. Heating & Cooling (icon: thermometer)

- Heating system turns on and heats every room | Turn the thermostat up and walk through the house. Every room should warm up. | High | HVAC technician | B A W
- Air conditioning turns on and cools (warm weather only) | Don't run the AC when it's cold outside — you can damage it. | Medium | HVAC technician | B A
- Age of furnace/boiler and AC unit is known | Ask the seller or look at the labels. Older equipment means a replacement may be coming. | Medium | HVAC technician | B
- Filter is clean | A dirty filter makes the system work harder. Easy fix, often ignored. | Low | Homeowner / HVAC technician | A W
- System has been serviced in the last year | A yearly tune-up catches small problems before the coldest night of the year. | Medium | HVAC technician | A W
- No rust, soot or burning smell around the furnace | Soot or a burning smell around a gas furnace means call a technician — don't wait. | High | HVAC technician | B A W
- Outdoor AC unit is level and clear of plants and debris | Give it at least two feet of breathing room. | Low | HVAC technician | A
- Fireplace and chimney were cleaned/inspected recently | Creosote buildup in a chimney is a fire risk. | High | Chimney sweep | B A W

## 11. Kitchen (icon: pot)

- Appliances turn on and work | Run the stove burners, oven, dishwasher and garbage disposal. | Low | Appliance repair | B
- No leaks under the sink or behind the dishwasher | Look and feel. Swollen cabinet floors are a giveaway. | Medium | Plumber | B A
- Range hood works and vents outside if possible | A hood that just blows air back into the room is better than nothing, but not by much. | Low | Electrician / HVAC technician | B
- Outlets by the counter are GFCI protected | Press test and reset on each one. | High | Electrician | B A
- Cabinets and drawers open, close and are attached solidly | Loose upper cabinets are a hazard. | Low | Carpenter | B

## 12. Bathrooms (icon: bath)

- Floor is solid around the toilet and tub (no soft spots) | Press down with your foot. Soft floor near a toilet means a slow leak has been going on. | High | Plumber / carpenter | B A
- Caulk and grout around tub and shower are intact | Cracked caulk lets water behind the tile, where you can't see it. | Medium | Handyman / tile contractor | B A
- Tiles are not loose or cracked | Tap gently. Hollow sounding tiles can mean water damage behind them. | Medium | Tile contractor | B
- Exhaust fan works and actually pulls air | Hold a piece of toilet paper up to it. It should stick. | Medium | Electrician | B A
- No mold on ceiling or walls | Small spots on a bathroom ceiling usually mean poor ventilation. | Medium | Handyman / mold remediation | B A
- Hot water arrives in a reasonable time | Waiting forever for hot water is mostly annoying — but note it. | Low | Plumber | B

## 13. Inside Rooms (icon: door)

- No water stains on ceilings | Stains under bathrooms or near outside walls usually point straight at the source. | High | Roofer / plumber | B A
- No large cracks in walls or ceilings | Hairline cracks are common. Wide or growing cracks are worth asking about. | Medium | Structural engineer | B
- Floors have no soft spots, buckling or major damage | Buckled wood floors often mean water. | Medium | Flooring contractor | B
- Stairs are solid with secure handrails | Every staircase should have a handrail you can grip. | High | Carpenter | B A
- Older home (built before 1978): painted surfaces noted for possible lead paint | Peeling paint in older homes can contain lead, which is dangerous especially for kids. Get it tested before sanding. | High | Lead paint inspector | B
- Older home (built before 1980s): possible asbestos materials noted (do not disturb) | Some old floor tiles, pipe wrap and popcorn ceilings can contain asbestos. Test before any renovation. | High | Asbestos testing professional | B

## 14. Safety (icon: shield)

- Smoke alarms in every bedroom, outside sleeping areas and on every level | Press the test button on every one. They don't last forever — most should be replaced about every 10 years. | High | Homeowner / electrician | B A W
- Carbon monoxide alarms near sleeping areas (if gas appliances or attached garage) | Carbon monoxide has no smell. An alarm is the only warning you get. | High | Homeowner / electrician | B A W
- Fire extinguisher in the kitchen | Check the gauge is in the green. | Medium | Homeowner | A W
- Dryer vent is clean and vents outside | Lint buildup in dryer vents causes house fires. Clean it once a year. | High | Dryer vent cleaning | B A W
- Bedroom windows can be opened as an escape route | Every bedroom needs a way out besides the door. | High | Window contractor | B
- Gas smell anywhere? (should be none) | If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas company from outside. Don't flip switches. | High | Gas utility company | B A W

## 15. Garage (icon: car)

- Garage door reverses when it hits something | Put a roll of paper towels under the door and close it. It should stop and go back up. | High | Garage door technician | B A
- Door between garage and house closes on its own and is solid | That door is there to slow down a fire and keep car fumes out. | Medium | Carpenter | B
- No cracks or water in the garage floor beyond normal wear | Small cracks are normal. Big heaving or water coming in is not. | Low | Concrete contractor | B
- Garage outlets are GFCI protected | Press test and reset. | Medium | Electrician | B A

## Questions to ask the seller (Buying a home mode — PDF page)

- How old is the roof, and has it ever leaked?
- Has the basement or crawlspace ever had water in it?
- How old are the furnace, AC and water heater? Do you have service records?
- Have there been any repairs to the foundation or structure?
- Have there been any insurance claims on the house?
- Has the house been tested for radon? What was the result?
- Any problems with the sewer line, septic or well?
- What renovations were done, and were permits pulled?
- Any pest problems, past or present (termites, mice, other)?
- What are the average monthly utility bills?
- Is there anything you know about the house that I should know?
