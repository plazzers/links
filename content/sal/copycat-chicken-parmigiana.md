Chicken parm is on every Italian-American menu in the country, and for good reason. A golden, crunchy cutlet, bright tomato sauce, a blanket of melted mozzarella. When it's done right, it's one of the best plates in the whole cuisine. When it's done wrong, it's a soggy cutlet sitting in a puddle.

The restaurant-style version you're thinking of is very doable at home. It takes about 45 minutes, costs roughly $3 a plate, and the crust stays crunchy, if you follow one rule I'll tell you about in a minute.

Here's what makes the restaurant version work, what goes in it, and how to avoid the mistakes that turn a great parm into a sad one.

## What makes the restaurant version work

A good chicken parm is a game of textures. Crisp against soft. Hot against melty. Here's what the good kitchens get right:

- **Thin, even cutlets.** Thick chicken breasts take forever to cook through, and by then the crust is too dark. Restaurants slice the breast in half horizontally so every piece is thin and cooks fast.
- **A crunchy coating.** Panko mixed with grated parmesan gives you a crust that's light, crisp and savory.
- **Real frying.** A shallow pan of hot oil, not a sad little drizzle. The oil is what makes the crust golden all over.
- **Sauce on top, cheese on top, quick melt.** The cutlet goes under a hot broiler or into a hot oven just long enough to melt the cheese.

That's the whole show. No secret powder. Just care.

## Ingredients at a glance

Here's the lineup, by name only. Exact amounts and the full step-by-step are in Sal's Restaurant Copycat Cookbook and Sal's Kitchen App.

- **Chicken breasts** – halved horizontally into thin cutlets.
- **Flour** – the first coat, so the egg has something to grab.
- **Eggs** – beaten, the glue.
- **Panko breadcrumbs** – lighter and crunchier than regular breadcrumbs.
- **Grated parmesan** – mixed into the panko for flavor and a deeper golden color.
- **Oil for frying** – something neutral with a high smoke point.
- **Marinara** – homemade if you can. My [Sunday marinara sauce](../sunday-marinara-sauce/) is the one I use, and the full recipe is free.
- **Shredded low-moisture mozzarella** – the kind that melts and stretches without going watery.
- **Fresh basil** – torn over the top at the end.

## Sal's key technique tips

### Sauce on top only. Never underneath.

This is the rule. Sauce under the chicken turns that beautiful crust into wet cardboard. You fried it to make it crunchy, so why would you make it sit in a pool? Spoon the sauce over the top, add the cheese, melt it, and serve extra sauce on the side or on the pasta. When I see a cutlet swimming in sauce, I know it was made by somebody who doesn't care.

### Flour, egg, crumb, in that order

The classic breading line. Flour dries the surface. Egg sticks to the flour. Crumbs stick to the egg. Skip the flour and the coating slides off in the pan. Use one hand for dry and the other for wet, and your fingers won't turn into breaded drumsticks.

### Hot oil, not too many at once

If the oil isn't hot enough, the crust soaks it up and goes greasy. If you crowd the pan, the temperature drops and you get the same problem. Fry a couple of cutlets at a time and let the oil come back up between batches.

### Rest them on a rack

Out of the pan, onto a wire rack, not a paper towel. Steam gets trapped under the cutlet on a paper towel, and the bottom goes soft. A rack keeps it crisp all around.

### Low-moisture mozzarella for the top

Fresh mozzarella is beautiful on a pizza margherita, but it releases water as it melts. On a crispy cutlet, that water is your enemy. Low-moisture mozzarella melts clean and browns in spots.

## Common mistakes

1. **Thick cutlets.** The outside burns while the middle is still raw. Slice or pound them thin and even.
2. **Sauce underneath.** I'm saying it twice because it matters.
3. **Breading that falls off.** Usually a skipped flour step or chicken that was still wet. Pat the chicken dry first.
4. **Cold oil.** Greasy, pale crust. Wait for the oil to shimmer.
5. **Too long under the broiler.** You only need the cheese melted and bubbly. Walk away and it burns fast.
6. **A watery sauce.** A thin sauce runs everywhere. Simmer your marinara until it's thick enough to sit on a spoon.

## Make-ahead tips for a busy night

Chicken parm has a few parts, so split the work. Make the marinara a day or two ahead; it tastes even better the next day. Bread the cutlets in the morning and keep them on a rack in the fridge, uncovered, so the coating dries a little and fries up even crunchier. Then at dinner time it's just fry, top, melt, serve.

## Serving ideas

- **Spaghetti with marinara** on the side. The classic, and the reason you make extra sauce.
- **A crisp green salad** with a sharp vinaigrette to balance all that cheese.
- **Garlic breadsticks** for the full restaurant night. My [copycat breadsticks](../copycat-garlic-butter-breadsticks/) get brushed twice, the way the chains do it.
- **Sandwich tomorrow.** Leftover chicken parm on a toasted roll is one of life's great pleasures. Reheat it in the oven, not the microwave, so the crust comes back.
- **Roasted vegetables** like zucchini or green beans if you want something lighter alongside.
