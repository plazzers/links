This is the sauce that makes cheap food taste expensive. Butter, garlic, a splash of chicken broth, a squeeze of lemon, fresh parsley. Ten minutes, about $1.50 a batch, and it goes on almost everything: steak, shrimp, chicken, fish, potatoes, vegetables, bread.

Here's my favorite trick with it. Spoon it over a cheap sirloin, and your family will think you went to the steakhouse. That glossy, garlicky butter on top is a big part of what makes a steakhouse steak feel special.

## Why this sauce works

Melted butter alone is nice. But it's one-note, and it separates into fat and milky bits. This sauce fixes both problems:

- **Garlic** gives it that big, savory aroma.
- **Chicken broth** adds depth and helps make it a sauce instead of just melted butter.
- **Lemon** cuts the richness so you want another bite instead of feeling heavy.
- **Parsley** brings color and a fresh, grassy note at the end.

The result is rich but bright. That balance is why restaurant butter sauces taste so good, and why a plain pat of butter doesn't do the same thing.

## Technique: keep the garlic golden, not brown

### Medium-low heat, the whole time

Butter has milk solids in it, and they burn faster than oil. Garlic burns fast too. Put them together over high heat and you get a bitter, brown mess in seconds. Melt the butter over medium-low, and keep it there.

### Fragrant, not brown

When the garlic goes in, stay at the stove. You're looking for the moment the kitchen smells amazing and the garlic is soft and pale gold at the edges. That's it. If it turns brown, it'll taste bitter, and the whole sauce tastes bitter with it. Start again; it's only butter and garlic.

### Mince it fine

Finely minced garlic flavors the butter quickly and evenly. Big chunks need longer to soften, and by then the small bits have burned.

### Lemon and parsley at the end

Lemon juice gets dull if it cooks too long. Parsley loses its color. Both go in right at the end, then season with salt and pepper and taste. A pinch more salt usually makes it sing.

### Serve warm

As it cools, butter firms up. Serve it warm, and if it sits too long, warm it gently again over low heat with a little stir.

## Common mistakes

1. **High heat.** Burnt butter solids and bitter garlic. Medium-low, always.
2. **Walking away.** This sauce takes ten minutes. Stay with it for all ten.
3. **Bottled minced garlic.** It's convenient, but it often tastes sour and flat next to fresh cloves.
4. **Skipping the lemon.** Without it, the sauce is heavy. A little acid makes it taste like a restaurant sauce.
5. **Forgetting to season.** Butter sauces need salt to taste like anything. Taste and adjust.

## Variations

- **Herb butter sauce.** Add chopped fresh thyme or rosemary with the garlic for steak; dill or chives for fish.
- **Spicy.** A pinch of red pepper flakes with the garlic.
- **Scampi-style.** Swap the broth for a splash of dry white wine and let it bubble for a minute. Perfect for shrimp and pasta.
- **Parmesan garlic butter.** Stir in a little grated Parmigiano at the end, then toss with hot pasta.
- **Brown butter version.** For the brave: cook the butter alone until it smells nutty and turns light brown, take it off the heat, then add the garlic. Watch it like a hawk.
- **Compound butter.** Skip the broth, mash garlic, parsley and lemon zest into softened butter, roll it into a log and chill. Slice a coin onto a hot steak.

## What to put it on

### Steak

The big one. Season a cheap cut like sirloin well, sear it hard in a hot pan, let it rest, then spoon the warm garlic butter over the top as you slice it. Restaurants charge a fortune for this exact move. If you're curious how steakhouses price these plates, I talk about it in [menu price tricks](../menu-price-tricks/).

### Shrimp and seafood

Toss sautéed shrimp in it, spoon it over baked salmon or white fish, or use it as a dip for crab legs or lobster. Want to go further? My [copycat shrimp scampi](../copycat-shrimp-scampi/) is built on the same idea, with wine, lemon and linguine.

### Bread

Brush it on split rolls or a sliced baguette and toast under the broiler for quick garlic bread. Or brush it over warm [copycat breadsticks](../copycat-garlic-butter-breadsticks/).

### Vegetables and potatoes

Roasted potatoes, green beans, broccoli, asparagus, corn on the cob, mushrooms. A spoonful turns a boring side into the thing everyone fights over.

### Pasta and rice

Toss it with hot spaghetti, a handful of grated cheese and a splash of pasta water. Dinner in fifteen minutes.

## Storing and cost

### Storing it

Leftover sauce goes into a small covered container in the fridge, where it will firm up like flavored butter. That's fine. Scoop out what you need and melt it gently over low heat, or just put a spoonful on top of something hot and let it melt there. Use it within a few days.
