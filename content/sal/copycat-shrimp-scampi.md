Shrimp scampi is the dish people order at the Italian and seafood chains because it feels a little fancy. Pink shrimp, linguine, a glossy sauce of garlic, butter, white wine and lemon. It's bright, it's rich, and it's on the table fast.

Here's the thing. Restaurants charge a premium for it, but it's one of the easiest dishes you can make at home. About 20 minutes. Roughly $4 a plate. And you can make it better, because you're cooking one pan for your family, not two hundred plates on a Friday night.

The one thing standing between you and a great scampi? Overcooked shrimp. Let's fix that first.

## What makes the restaurant version work

A great scampi balances four things:

- **Garlic** that's sweet and fragrant, never burnt.
- **Butter and olive oil** together. The oil lets you cook a little hotter without the butter burning; the butter gives you that rich, silky finish.
- **Acid** from white wine and lemon, so the sauce tastes bright instead of heavy.
- **Tender shrimp.** Snappy and juicy, not tight little rubber balls.

The pasta gets tossed right in the pan, so every strand picks up the garlic butter. That's the difference between a scampi and shrimp sitting on top of plain noodles.

## Ingredients at a glance

- **Linguine** – flat, long noodles that hold a thin sauce well. Spaghetti works in a pinch.
- **Shrimp** – peeled and deveined. Frozen raw shrimp is completely fine; thaw it in the fridge or under cold running water.
- **Butter and olive oil** – the team.
- **Garlic** – sliced thin, not minced. Slices are gentler and harder to burn.
- **Red pepper flakes** – just a little warmth in the background.
- **Dry white wine** – or chicken broth if you don't cook with wine.
- **Fresh lemon juice** – squeezed from a real lemon. The bottle tastes flat.
- **Chopped parsley** – to finish.

## Sal's key technique tips

### Take the shrimp out early

This is the rule. Shrimp cook in a couple of minutes, and they keep cooking after they leave the heat. So sear them quickly, pull them out of the pan when they're just turning pink and barely opaque, and set them aside. They go back in at the very end and finish cooking in the hot sauce. Rubber shrimp is the number one restaurant crime. Don't commit it at home.

### Dry the shrimp first

Pat them dry with paper towels before they hit the pan. Wet shrimp steam instead of searing, and they release water into your sauce.

### Gentle heat for the garlic

Once the shrimp are out, lower the heat. Thin garlic slices should go soft and golden at the edges, never dark brown. Burnt garlic is bitter, and there's no saving the pan after that.

### Reduce the wine

Let the wine bubble for a minute or two before you add the rest. You want to cook off the sharp alcohol taste and concentrate the flavor. If you skip this, the sauce tastes boozy and thin.

### Finish with cold butter and pasta water

A few pieces of butter swirled in at the end, off the heat, plus a splash of starchy pasta water, gives you a sauce that's glossy and clings to the noodles instead of sliding off.

## Common mistakes

1. **Cooking the shrimp all the way in the first step.** By the time the sauce is done, they're tough. Pull them early.
2. **Crowding the pan.** Too many shrimp at once and they steam. Cook in batches if your pan is small.
3. **Browned garlic.** Lower the heat before it goes in.
4. **Bottled lemon juice.** It's the main bright note in the dish, so use a real lemon.
5. **Draining all the pasta water.** Save a mug of it. It's the glue for your sauce.
6. **Plating and waiting.** Like alfredo, scampi is best the minute it's tossed. Have everyone at the table.

## Shopping questions: shrimp and wine

### Fresh or frozen shrimp?

Here's a little secret: a lot of the "fresh" shrimp at the seafood counter was frozen before, then thawed for display. So buying frozen raw shrimp yourself is often the smarter move. You thaw it when you're ready, and you usually pay less. Look for shrimp that's raw, peeled and deveined, and skip the pre-cooked kind. Pre-cooked shrimp gets tough the moment it hits a hot pan.

### What about the wine?

People ask me this a lot. The wine in scampi is there for acid and depth, not to make it boozy. Pick a dry white you'd happily drink, something crisp and not too oaky. If it tastes bad in the glass, it tastes bad in the pan. Sweet wines make the sauce taste odd and sugary, so skip those.

No wine in the house, or you'd rather not cook with it? Chicken broth with an extra squeeze of lemon does the job. The sauce is a little softer and rounder, but still delicious. Nobody at the table is going to send it back.

## Serving ideas

- **Crusty bread** for the sauce at the bottom of the bowl. Or go all in with [garlic butter breadsticks](../copycat-garlic-butter-breadsticks/).
- **A simple arugula salad** with lemon and olive oil.
- **Roasted asparagus or broccolini** alongside, or tossed right into the pasta.
- **Skip the pasta** and serve the shrimp and sauce over rice, or just with bread, as a starter.

## The short version

- Dry the shrimp, sear them fast, and take them out early.
- Gentle heat for thin-sliced garlic.
- Reduce the wine, add real lemon, finish with butter and pasta water.
- Shrimp back in at the very end. Tender, never rubber. Basta.
