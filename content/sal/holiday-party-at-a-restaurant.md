Every December, somebody gets volunteered. "You're good at this, you book the place." And suddenly you're the one calling restaurants for the office party, the family get-together or the dinner with the old friends you only see once a year.

Booking a group at a restaurant during the holidays is not like booking a table for two. There are minimums, set menus, deposits, service charges and contracts with small print. None of it is a scam. Restaurants are busy in December and a big group is a big risk for them. But if you don't ask the right questions, the final bill can be a surprise for everyone.

So here's how group bookings work, and what to ask before you sign anything.

## Book early, and know your numbers

December weekends fill up fast, especially private rooms and big tables on Friday and Saturday nights. Before you call, have three things ready:

- **A headcount.** A rough one is fine, but know the range. Fifteen and forty are different conversations.
- **A date, and a backup date.** A weeknight is often easier to get, and sometimes the minimums are lower.
- **A budget per person**, including drinks, tax and tip. Decide this first. It makes every other choice easier.

And ask when the final headcount is due. Many places want a firm number a few days ahead, and that's the number you'll pay for, even if three people cancel with a cold.

## The minimum: the most important word in the contract

Many private rooms and big group areas don't charge a "room fee" as such. Instead, they have a **food and beverage minimum**: an amount your group has to spend. Reach it, and the room is effectively free. Fall short, and you pay the difference anyway.

Questions to ask:

- **What is the minimum, and does it change by night?** Saturdays are often higher than Tuesdays.
- **What counts toward it?** Food and drinks usually. Tax, service charges and tips usually don't.
- **What happens if we don't reach it?** Usually a charge for the difference, sometimes called a room fee.

That last one matters. A minimum that's easy for forty people can be a stretch for fifteen.

## Set menus and family style

For groups, many restaurants offer a set holiday menu: a fixed price per person for a few courses, sometimes with a choice of main dish. Or family style, where big platters go down the middle of the table.

This isn't a trick. A set menu lets the kitchen prep ahead and serve a big group fast and hot, which is better for you too. But read the per-person price carefully:

- **What's included?** Appetizers? Dessert? Coffee? Bread?
- **Are there upgrades?** "Add a shrimp course." "Upgrade to the steak." These add up per person.
- **Is there an option for guests who don't eat meat**, or who have allergies? Ask now, not on the night.
- **Do guests pick their main dish ahead of time** or at the table?

Same rule as in my guide to [restaurant upselling tricks](../restaurant-upselling-tricks/): decide on the extras before you sit down.

## Drinks: where group bills grow

Drinks are where a group bill can quietly double. I explain why in [the restaurant drink markup](../restaurant-drink-markup/), and with a big group it multiplies.

You usually have a few options:

- **Open bar**: everything goes on one tab, often with a time limit or a spending cap. Ask what's included and whether there's a cap.
- **Drink tickets**: each guest gets a set number of drinks covered.
- **Wine with dinner**: you choose a few bottles ahead of time and the staff pour.
- **Cash bar**: guests pay for their own drinks.

For an office party, a cap or tickets keep things predictable. For a family dinner, picking a couple of wines ahead and having water and soft drinks on the table works well. If you have a special bottle in mind, ask about the corkage policy first.

## Service charges, gratuity and fees

This is the line that surprises people. Many restaurants add an **automatic service charge or gratuity** to large parties or private events. Sometimes there's also an administrative or event fee.

Ask these questions plainly:

- **Is a service charge or gratuity added? What percentage?**
- **Does that service charge go to the staff?** Some places use the words differently, so it's fair to ask.
- **Is tax calculated before or after the service charge?**
- **Are there other fees?** Cake cutting, room setup, a microphone, extra staff, decorations.

Then, on the night, look at the bill before you add anything. If gratuity is already included, you don't need to tip twice, though a little extra for a server who handled your group beautifully is always a kind gesture.

## Deposits and cancellations

Holiday bookings often come with a deposit and a cancellation policy.

- **How much is the deposit, and is it refundable?**
- **Until what date can we cancel or change the headcount** without paying?
- **What if bad weather closes the roads?** Some places will move the date; ask.

Get the answers in writing. An email confirming the date, time, headcount, menu, minimum, service charges and deposit is enough. It protects you and the restaurant.

## Splitting the bill

If everyone is paying for themselves, sort this out at booking time, not at the end of the night.

- **Ask if separate checks are possible** for a group your size. Some places limit how many ways they can split a big bill.
- **If not, choose one person to pay** and collect from everyone after, or set a per-person amount in advance that covers food, drinks, tax and tip.
- **Tell the server at the start.** Asking at the end, when everyone has their coats on, makes for slow and messy math.

A simple per-person price, agreed ahead, avoids the classic problem where the people who had salad and water pay for the people who had the steak and three cocktails.

## The questions, all in one place

Before you sign or pay a deposit, ask:

- What's the food and beverage minimum, and what counts toward it?
- What's on the set menu, and what's extra?
- Are there options for guests with dietary needs?
- How do drinks work, and is there a cap?
- Is a service charge or gratuity added, and at what rate?
- Are there any other fees?
- How much is the deposit, and what's the cancellation policy?
- When is the final headcount due?
- Can you do separate checks for our group?
- Can I have all of this confirmed in writing?

## The short version

Book early with a headcount, a date and a budget per person. Ask about the minimum, what the set menu includes, how drinks work, what service charges and fees are added, the deposit and the cancellation rules. Sort out splitting the bill before anyone orders. Get it all in writing. Then relax and enjoy the party you planned. Mangia.
