There's a cup of chili on the menu at the square-burger fast food place that people order on cold days without even looking. Thick, beefy, a little sweet, beans in every spoonful, not too spicy. Comfort food in a paper cup.

You can make a big pot of it at home that's better than the cup. It takes about an hour and a half, most of it a slow simmer while the house starts to smell like a diner. It feeds eight people, and it works out to about $1.50 a bowl.

And here's the best part. It's even better the next day. Restaurants know this. Their chili is very often yesterday's chili, and for once they've got it right.

Let's talk about how to make yours taste like the real thing.

## What makes diner chili taste like diner chili

This isn't fancy competition chili with three kinds of dried peppers. It's the everyday kind, and it has a few things that make it recognizable.

- **Ground beef, browned well.** Not gray. Browned. That's where the deep, beefy flavor comes from.
- **The vegetables cooked down.** Onion, green bell pepper and celery, diced small so they melt into the chili instead of sitting in chunks.
- **A simple spice mix.** Chili powder, cumin, oregano, paprika and a little sugar. The sugar is the secret most home cooks skip. It rounds off the tomato and gives that slightly sweet fast food taste.
- **Two kinds of beans.** Kidney and pinto. Kidney beans hold their shape; pintos go soft and creamy and thicken everything.
- **Tomato sauce, not just tomatoes.** That's what gives it that smooth, thick body instead of a watery stew.

## The ingredients (names only)

Here's what goes into the pot. The exact amounts and the full method are in the cookbook.

- Ground beef
- Onion, green bell pepper and celery
- Garlic
- Chili powder, cumin, oregano, paprika and a little sugar
- Diced tomatoes and tomato sauce
- Kidney beans and pinto beans, drained
- Beef broth
- Salt and pepper

Nothing you can't find in any grocery store. Most of it may already be in your pantry.

## Sal's key tips

**Brown the beef in batches if your pot is small.** Crowd the pan and the meat steams instead of browning. Let it sit and get some color before you stir it. Those brown bits on the bottom are flavor. Don't leave them behind.

**Cook the vegetables in the beef fat.** After the beef is browned, the onion, pepper and celery go right into the same pot. They soak up the flavor and soften. Give them time, until the onion turns soft and see-through.

**Toast the spices.** Add the spices to the hot pot with the vegetables and stir them for about a minute before any liquid goes in. Warm oil wakes up dried spices. Same spices, much more flavor. Just keep them moving so they don't scorch.

**Simmer low and slow.** Once everything is in, bring it to a gentle bubble, then turn it down. A lazy simmer, partly covered, stirring now and then. This is when the beans soften, the sauce thickens and the flavors come together. Rushing this step is the difference between "chili" and "beef in tomato sauce."

**Taste at the end.** Salt last. The broth, the canned tomatoes and the beans all bring salt, so taste before you add more. A pinch more sugar if it's sharp, a little more chili powder if it's flat.

## Common mistakes

**Gray, steamed beef.** Covered above, but it's the most common one. Brown it.

**Too thin.** If it's soupy after simmering, leave the lid off for the last stretch and let it cook down. Mashing a spoonful of the pinto beans against the side of the pot thickens it too.

**Too spicy for the family.** Diner chili is mild. Start gentle; people who want heat can add hot sauce or crushed red pepper at the table. You can't take heat back out.

**Big chunks of vegetables.** Dice them small. In diner chili you should taste the vegetables, not chew around them.

**Serving it the same minute it's done.** Even 20 minutes off the heat with the lid on helps. Overnight is better.

## Why it's better the next day

As chili sits, the spices soak into the beans and meat and the whole pot gets thicker and rounder. So make it a day ahead when you can, especially for a party or game day.

Let it cool, put it in the fridge in shallow containers so it chills quickly, and reheat it gently on the stove with a splash of broth or water, stirring so the bottom doesn't stick. Chili also freezes very well. Freeze it in portions and you've got dinner on a busy weeknight.

## Toppings and how to serve it

The toppings are where it turns into a diner meal:

- Shredded cheddar
- Chopped raw onion or green onion
- A spoonful of sour cream
- Crackers on the side
- Hot sauce at the table for whoever wants it

Serve it in bowls with a basket of [cheddar garlic biscuits](../copycat-cheddar-garlic-biscuits/) or cornbread. Spoon it over a baked potato. Ladle it on hot dogs. Or put it next to [smash burgers](../smash-burgers-special-sauce/) for a full diner night at home.

For game day, set it out in a slow cooker on the warm setting with the toppings in little bowls and let everyone build their own. It's the easiest party food there is.
