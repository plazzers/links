The person who knows the most about a house is the person who lives in it. They know which corner of the basement got wet in the spring storm, how old the roof really is, and why the back bedroom is always cold. Asking good questions is free, takes a few minutes, and can save you from the most expensive surprises.

Below are the questions to ask the seller when buying a house, grouped by topic, with notes on why each one matters. Ask them through your agent or directly when appropriate, ask for answers in writing when you can, and compare what you hear with what the disclosure forms and your inspection show.

## How to ask (and why it matters)

A few habits make the answers more useful:

- **Ask open questions.** "Has the basement ever had water in it?" gets a better answer than "The basement's dry, right?"
- **Ask for paperwork.** Receipts, permits, warranties and service records turn a vague memory into facts.
- **Write it down,** with the date and who answered.
- **Don't treat answers as an inspection.** Sellers can only tell you what they know. A qualified home inspector and, where needed, licensed specialists still need to look.

Disclosure rules vary by state and country, so your agent can tell you what the seller is required to share.

## The roof and outside

1. **How old is the roof, and has it ever leaked?** Ask for the install date, the contractor, and any warranty. Asphalt shingle roofs commonly last roughly 20 to 30 years, so the age tells you a lot.
2. **Have the gutters or drainage ever been changed?** New downspout extensions, regrading or drains often follow a water problem.
3. **Any problems with the deck, porch or retaining walls?** Ask when they were built or last repaired.

## Water, basement and foundation

4. **Has the basement or crawlspace ever had water in it?** Ask when, how much, what caused it, and what was done.
5. **Is there a sump pump, and how often does it run?** A pump that runs constantly tells a story.
6. **Have there been any repairs to the foundation or structure?** If yes, ask for the engineer's report and the contractor's paperwork. See [foundation crack types](../foundation-crack-types/) for what different cracks can mean.
7. **Any history of mold, and was it professionally handled?**

## Heating, cooling and water heater

8. **How old are the furnace, AC and water heater?** Ask for service records too. Tank water heaters commonly last roughly 8 to 12 years.
9. **When were they last serviced?** A yearly service record is a good sign.
10. **Are there any rooms that are hard to heat or cool?** The answer can point to duct, insulation or equipment issues.
11. **What are the average monthly utility bills?** Ask for a year's range if you can — it says a lot about insulation and equipment.

## Plumbing, sewer, well and septic

12. **What type of water supply pipes does the house have, and have any been replaced?** Older galvanized steel or polybutylene pipe is worth asking about.
13. **Any problems with the sewer line, septic or well?** Backups, root problems, pumping history, water test results.
14. **Has the sewer line ever been scoped or replaced?** If not, consider a [sewer line camera inspection](../sewer-line-camera-inspection/), especially on an older house or one with big trees.
15. **Where is the main water shut-off?** Good to know on day one.

## Electrical

16. **Has the electrical panel or wiring been updated? When, and by whom?** Ask whether a licensed electrician did the work and whether permits were pulled.
17. **What size is the electrical service?** Older homes sometimes have small service that struggles with modern use.
18. **Do any breakers trip often?** Repeated tripping is worth an electrician's look.

## Safety and environmental questions

19. **Has the house been tested for radon? What was the result, and when?** The EPA recommends testing during a home sale and fixing the home if the level is 4 pCi/L or higher. If a mitigation system is installed, ask for its paperwork.
20. **Is there any known lead-based paint?** For homes built before 1978 in the US, sellers must disclose known lead-based paint hazards and provide available records, and buyers get an opportunity to test.
21. **Any known asbestos-containing materials, and has anything been tested or removed?** Ask for the lab results or abatement records.
22. **Any pest problems, past or present?** Termites, mice, bats or other wildlife — and any treatment records.

## Renovations, permits and history

23. **What renovations were done, and were permits pulled and closed?** This matters a lot on flips — see [how to spot a bad house flip](../how-to-spot-a-bad-house-flip/).
24. **Have there been any insurance claims on the house?** Water, fire, storm or sewer claims are worth knowing about.
25. **Why are you selling?** Not everyone will answer, and that's fine — but sometimes you'll learn something useful.
26. **Is there anything you know about the house that I should know?** Simple, but it gives an honest seller room to mention things the forms don't cover.

## What to do with the answers

Answers are a starting point, not the finish line:

- **Compare them with the disclosure form.** Inconsistencies are worth a polite follow-up.
- **Share them with your inspector.** Tell them about past water, repairs or anything that worried you, so they look closely there.
- **Call the right specialist** for anything structural, electrical, sewer or environmental. A licensed electrician, plumber, roofer or structural engineer can give an opinion the general inspection can't.
- **Use them to shape your offer** or your inspection contingency, with your agent's help.

Pair these questions with what you see in person — my list of [house red flags at a showing](../house-red-flags-at-a-showing/) covers what to look for while you're there.

## The short version

Ask about the roof, water, structure, systems, permits and environmental testing — and ask for paperwork, not just memories. Write the answers down, compare them with the disclosures, and hand them to your inspector. A seller's answers are useful, but they never replace a professional inspection.
