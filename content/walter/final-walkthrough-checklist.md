You found the house, made the offer, got through the inspection and the paperwork, and now closing day is close. There's one more step that's easy to rush: the final walk-through.

It's your last look at the house before you sign and it becomes yours. It isn't a second home inspection. It's a check that the house is in the condition you agreed to buy it in, that the agreed repairs were done, and that nothing new has gone wrong since you last saw it. Both the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau and Freddie Mac recommend doing one before you sign the closing papers.

Here's how to plan it and what to check, room by room.

## Why the final walk-through matters

Weeks can pass between your offer and closing. In that time, a lot can happen to a house:

- The sellers move out, and furniture and boxes scrape walls, floors and door frames on the way.
- Something breaks: a water heater, a furnace, a dishwasher.
- A storm, a frozen pipe or a slow leak causes damage nobody noticed.
- Repairs you negotiated are done, done partly, or not done at all.
- Items that were supposed to stay with the house leave with the moving truck.

Once you close, fixing any of this is mostly up to you. Before you close, you still have a chance to ask for it to be handled. That's the whole point of the walk-through.

## When to schedule it and what to bring

**Timing.** Freddie Mac suggests a walk-through within about 24 hours before closing. Ideally, the sellers have already moved out, so you can see the floors and walls that furniture was covering. Your agent will help schedule it.

**Bring:**

- **Your purchase contract** and any repair agreement or amendment, so you know exactly what was promised.
- **Your inspection report**, to check the items that were supposed to be repaired.
- **Receipts or paperwork for repairs**, if the seller has provided them. Ask your agent to request them ahead of time.
- **A phone charger or a small plug-in outlet tester**, to check that outlets work.
- **A flashlight**, for under sinks, closets and the basement.
- **Your phone** for photos and notes.
- **Time.** Plan for an hour or so. Don't squeeze it in on the way to the closing table.

## Agreed repairs come first

Start with the list of repairs the seller agreed to. For each one:

- **Look at the work itself.** Is it finished? Does it look like it was done properly?
- **Ask for receipts, warranties or permits**, especially for roof, electrical, plumbing, heating or structural work. Work done by a licensed contractor should come with paperwork.
- **Compare it to the inspection report.** If the report said "have a licensed electrician evaluate the panel," a new cover plate isn't the same thing.

If you're not sure whether a repair was done correctly, you can ask your home inspector or the right contractor to come back and look before you close. Talk to your agent about timing.

## Room-by-room: what to test

You're not inspecting the house again, but you are making sure everything still works. Go through it in an order that makes sense, so nothing is skipped.

### Kitchen

- **Run the faucet**, hot and cold, and look under the sink with a flashlight for drips or water stains.
- **Turn on each burner** and the oven briefly.
- **Run the dishwasher** for a moment, and the garbage disposal if there is one.
- **Check the refrigerator** is cold, if it's staying with the house.
- **Turn on the range hood** fan and light.

### Bathrooms

- **Run every faucet and shower**, and check the water gets hot.
- **Flush each toilet** and watch that it refills and stops.
- **Look under the sinks** and around the toilet base for water or soft flooring.
- **Turn on the exhaust fans.**

### Heating, cooling and water

- **Turn the heat up** a few degrees and make sure warm air or heat comes on. In summer, check the air conditioning.
- **Check the water heater** area for water on the floor or rust at the base.
- **Look at the electrical panel** to be sure nothing looks newly scorched, wet or rusty. Don't open the panel cover; just look.

### Electrical and lighting

- **Flip every light switch.** Note any that don't work. It may be a bulb, or it may not.
- **Plug your charger or tester into outlets** in each room, especially any that were part of repairs.
- **Press TEST and RESET** on GFCI outlets in the kitchen, bathrooms, garage and outside. Our [GFCI outlets explained](../gfci-outlets-explained/) guide shows how.
- **Test the smoke and CO alarms.** You'll want to do this on move-in day anyway.

### Walls, floors, ceilings and windows

- **Look where the furniture used to be.** Scratches, holes, stains or damage that were hidden before.
- **Look up at the ceilings** for new water stains.
- **Open and close windows and doors**, and check that locks work.
- **Open the garage door** with the opener and make sure it reverses when something is in its way.

### Basement, attic and outside

- **Check the basement or crawlspace** for new water, dampness or a musty smell. Our [signs of water in a basement](../signs-of-water-in-basement/) guide covers what to look for.
- **Run the sump pump test** if there is one: lift the float gently and listen for the pump.
- **Walk around the outside.** Look for new damage to siding, gutters, fences or the roof edge you can see from the ground.

## Check what stays and what goes

Your contract says what conveys with the house. Make sure it's all there:

- **Appliances** listed in the contract.
- **Fixtures** like light fixtures, ceiling fans, window coverings and mounted shelves.
- **Remotes, keys and codes**: garage door openers, mailbox keys, alarm codes, smart thermostat or lock accounts.
- **Manuals and paperwork** for appliances and systems, if the sellers have them.

And check what was supposed to leave. The CFPB notes the house should be in the condition you agreed to. Leftover furniture, paint cans, trash or junk in the garage and attic can take a lot of time to clear.

## What to do if something is wrong

Small things are normal: a burned-out bulb, a nail hole where a picture hung. Bigger things are worth raising right away.

1. **Take clear photos** and write down exactly what you found.
2. **Tell your agent immediately**, before closing. The CFPB says to contact the seller right away if agreed repairs weren't made.
3. **Ask what your options are.** Depending on your contract and where you live, that may mean the seller fixes it before closing, a credit or money held back at closing, or a short delay. Your agent and, if you have one, your real estate attorney can explain what applies to you.
4. **Don't feel rushed into signing.** The CFPB's advice is not to sign anything until you're satisfied the documents and the deal match what you agreed to.

## Final walk-through checklist

- Agreed repairs done, with receipts, warranties and permits
- All faucets, toilets and showers working, no leaks under sinks
- Water gets hot; no water around the water heater
- Heat (or AC) turns on
- All lights, outlets and GFCIs work
- Appliances that stay with the house work
- No new damage to walls, floors, ceilings or windows
- Basement or crawlspace dry
- Garage door opener works and reverses
- Keys, remotes, codes and manuals handed over
- Agreed items left behind; everything else gone

## The short version

The final walk-through is your last chance to make sure you're getting the house you agreed to buy. Schedule it close to closing, bring your contract and inspection report, check the agreed repairs first, then test water, heat, lights, outlets and appliances room by room. Look for new damage, make sure what stays is still there, and raise any real problem with your agent before you sign.
