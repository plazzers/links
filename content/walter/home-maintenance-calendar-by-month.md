A house doesn't need a lot of attention at once. It needs a little attention, regularly. A home maintenance calendar spreads the jobs across the year so no single month feels like a project — and so the important ones, like heating tune-ups and gutter cleaning, happen *before* you need them instead of after.

Below is a month-by-month plan, January to December, written for a typical U.S. house with four seasons. If you live somewhere warm, shift the "before winter" jobs later and the "before summer" jobs earlier. The order matters more than the exact month.

Each month has a few small jobs you can do yourself and, where it makes sense, a reminder to book a pro.

## Every month, no matter what

Some checks belong on every month's list. They take about ten minutes together:

- **Test smoke and CO alarms.** Press the button on each one. NFPA recommends testing smoke alarms monthly.
- **Press TEST and RESET on GFCI outlets** in the kitchen, bathrooms, garage, laundry and outside.
- **Look under sinks** for drips or soft cabinet floors.
- **Check the furnace or AC filter** and change it when it's dirty. Many filters need changing every one to three months.

The [monthly house walk](../monthly-house-walk/) turns these into a quick ten-minute route through the house.

The sections below explain the reasoning behind each season, and where the line is between a DIY check and a pro.

## January to March: inside jobs and planning

Winter is a good time for the indoor checks you skip the rest of the year.

- **Smoke and CO alarm dates.** Look at the back of each one. NFPA says smoke alarms should be replaced every 10 years. CO alarms list their own replacement date.
- **Radon.** The EPA recommends testing every home, and winter, with windows closed, is a common time to do it. If the result is 4 pCi/L or higher, the EPA recommends fixing the home — call a qualified radon mitigation contractor. See [radon basics for homeowners](../radon-basics-for-homeowners/).
- **Water heater temperature.** Energy.gov suggests about 120°F for most homes — hot enough, with less scalding risk. Check it at the tap with a cooking thermometer, and have a plumber adjust it if needed.
- **Main water shut-off.** Find it and turn it gently. If it's stiff, don't force it; ask a plumber.
- **Ice dams.** In cold climates, watch the roof edge from the ground. Use a roof rake or call a roofer — stay off a snowy roof.

## April to June: water, drainage and getting ready for summer

Spring is all about water — snowmelt, heavy rain, and what winter did to your pipes.

- **Walk the outside.** Look at the foundation, siding, grading and the roof from the ground.
- **Gutters and downspouts.** Clean them, and make sure water exits several feet from the house. If your roof is high or steep, hire it out.
- **Outdoor faucets.** Turn them back on and check inside the wall behind each one for leaks.
- **Sump pump.** Pour a bucket of water in and make sure it runs. Details in [how to test a sump pump](../how-to-test-a-sump-pump/).
- **AC tune-up.** Book it in spring, before the first heat wave.
- **Deck and railings.** Shake every railing and look where the deck attaches to the house. If anything moves, stay off it and call a carpenter.

The [spring home maintenance checklist](../spring-home-maintenance-checklist/) goes into each of these in more detail.

## July to September: summer checks and booking fall service

Summer is quieter for maintenance, which makes it the right time to get ahead of fall.

- **Basement humidity.** Below about 60% helps keep mold and musty smells away. A simple humidity meter tells you; a dehumidifier helps.
- **Electrical panel.** Look and listen only. Scorch marks, buzzing or a burning smell mean call a licensed electrician. Never open the cover.
- **Chimney.** If you have a fireplace, book a chimney sweep in late summer, before everyone else does.
- **Heating tune-up.** Book it in September. Energy.gov notes that regular maintenance helps furnaces and boilers run safely and efficiently.
- **Dryer vent.** Have the duct cleaned once a year. Lint buildup is a fire risk.

## October to December: get ready for winter

Fall is the busiest stretch, because everything has to be ready before the first freeze.

- **Fire escape plan.** October is Fire Prevention Month — a good reminder to walk through two ways out of every bedroom.
- **Caulk, weatherstripping and pest gaps.** Seal small openings before mice move in for the winter.
- **Gutters again,** after the leaves are down.
- **Outdoor faucets off,** hoses disconnected, exposed pipes insulated.
- **Very cold nights.** Know which sinks are on outside walls, so you can open the cabinet doors and let a faucet drip.

The [winterize your house checklist](../winterize-your-house-checklist/) covers this season step by step.

## The short version

- Do the four quick safety checks every month.
- Spring and fall are for water: gutters, downspouts, faucets and sump pump.
- Book heating service in early fall and AC service in spring.
- Use winter for indoor checks, alarm dates and radon testing.
- When something involves electrical panels, gas, roofs or structure, call a licensed pro.
