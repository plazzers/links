Gutters have one job: catch the water coming off your roof and send it far enough away that it can't soak the ground next to your foundation. When they clog, overflow or dump water at the corner of the house, that water looks for the easiest way in — and that's usually the basement or crawlspace.

Fall is the most important time to deal with them. Leaves come down, then winter rain and snowmelt arrive. A clean gutter system and a few feet of downspout extension do more to keep a basement dry than almost anything else you can do yourself.

This guide covers how to clean gutters safely, how to check that the water actually ends up where it should, and when the job belongs to a pro.

## Why gutters and drainage matter so much

Most of the expensive problems in a house come back to water. A clogged gutter spills over the front edge, right along the foundation. Do that every rain for a few years and you can get wet basement walls, musty smells, mold, rotted trim and soil that settles or washes out.

The EPA's guidance on mold puts it simply: control moisture and you control mold. Outside, that starts with gutters, downspouts and the slope of the ground.

You don't need to be handy to check any of this. You need a dry afternoon, a rainy afternoon, and your eyes.

## Ladder safety first

Gutter cleaning is one of the most common reasons homeowners end up on a ladder, and ladders are where people get hurt. Before you start:

- **Only do it yourself on a single-story house with level ground** and a roof edge you can reach comfortably from a ladder.
- **Use a sturdy extension ladder** in good condition, set on firm, level ground. Extend it above the gutter line so you have something to hold as you step.
- **Keep your hips between the rails.** Move the ladder often instead of reaching to the side.
- **Have someone with you** to steady the ladder and hand things up.
- **Stay off the roof.** Don't walk the roof to reach the gutters.
- **Look up for power lines** before you raise a ladder, and keep well clear of them.
- **Skip it in wind, rain or frost.**

**When to call a pro instead:** if the house is two stories or higher, the roof is steep, the ground is sloped or soft, or you just don't feel steady up there. A gutter cleaning service has the right equipment and does this every day. There's no prize for doing it yourself.

## How to clean the gutters

Once you're set up safely:

1. **Wear gloves.** Gutter debris often hides sharp screws and broken bits of metal.
2. **Scoop out leaves and gunk** by hand or with a plastic scoop, working toward the downspout. Drop it onto a tarp below to save on cleanup.
3. **Flush with a hose.** Run water from the far end toward the downspout. It should flow steadily and drain without pooling.
4. **Clear the downspout.** If water backs up, the downspout is clogged. Flush it from the bottom first. A stubborn clog may need a plumber's snake or a pro.
5. **Look at the gutters while you're there.** Note sagging sections, loose hangers, gaps at the seams, and gutters pulling away from the fascia board.
6. **Check what came out.** Lots of sand-like granules in the gutters can mean your asphalt shingles are wearing out. The [how long does a roof last](../how-long-does-a-roof-last/) guide explains what that means.

Gutter guards can cut down on cleaning, but they still need checking. Leaves and seeds can pile up on top.

## Downspouts: where the water actually goes

Clean gutters don't help much if the downspout empties right at the foundation.

- **Each downspout should discharge several feet from the house.** A common rule of thumb is roughly 4 to 6 feet.
- **Add an extension or splash block** if it doesn't. This is one of the simplest, most effective fixes on any house.
- **Make sure the water flows away** after it leaves the extension — not back toward the house or into a neighbor's yard.
- **Underground drains** (downspouts that go into a pipe in the ground) can clog or collapse without any sign. If the gutter overflows at the downspout during heavy rain, the underground line may be blocked. A drainage contractor or plumber can check it.

## Grading and the ground around your house

The ground right next to the house should slope away on every side. Walk around and look at each corner. Mulch beds, settled soil and old window wells are the usual trouble spots.

- **Low spots and puddles** that sit next to the foundation after rain are water looking for a way in.
- **Mulch and soil** shouldn't be piled up against siding. Keep a gap so wood isn't in constant contact with damp ground.
- **Window wells** should be clear of leaves and drain freely.
- **Hard surfaces** like patios and walks should tilt away from the house too.

Small fixes — raking soil back into a low spot, pulling mulch back from the siding — you can do yourself. Re-grading a yard, adding a French drain or fixing a patio that slopes toward the house belong to a landscaper, grading contractor or drainage contractor.

## Check it in the rain

The best drainage check is free: **walk around the house during a steady rain** with an umbrella. You'll see in ten minutes what you'd never spot on a dry day.

- Are any gutters overflowing over the front edge?
- Is water pouring out of a seam or corner?
- Do the downspouts carry water away, or is it pooling at the foundation?
- Is water running toward the house across a patio or walkway?

Then go inside and check the basement or crawlspace a few hours later. Our article on [signs of water in a basement](../signs-of-water-in-basement/) shows what to look for. If you have a sump pump, make sure it's running when it should — see [how to test a sump pump](../how-to-test-a-sump-pump/).

## When to call a pro

Call a professional if you see:

- **Gutters pulling away** from the house or sagging badly — a gutter contractor.
- **Rot in the fascia boards** behind the gutters — a carpenter or roofer.
- **Roof damage, missing shingles or loose flashing** — a roofer. Don't go up to look.
- **Water in the basement after rain** even with clean gutters — a waterproofing contractor.
- **New or widening foundation cracks** — a structural engineer.

## The short version

Clean the gutters once the leaves are down, but only from a ladder on a low, simple roof — anything high or steep is a job for a gutter service. Make sure every downspout sends water several feet from the house, the ground slopes away, and nothing pools against the foundation. Then walk around in the next good rain and watch where the water really goes.
