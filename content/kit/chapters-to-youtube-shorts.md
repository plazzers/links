If your long videos already have chapters, you have a ready-made list of Shorts ideas. Each chapter is a small, self-contained piece of the topic, and many of them can stand on their own as a vertical clip. This guide shows you how to go from a chapter list to a planned batch of Shorts, with a hook, on-screen text and a title for each one. It works the same whether you narrate yourself, use an AI voice or work with an avatar.

## Why chapters make good Shorts

Chapters are already split by idea, which is exactly what a Short needs: one idea, delivered fast. When you plan Shorts from chapters instead of from scratch:

- You reuse research and script work you have already done.
- Each Short naturally points back to a longer video that goes deeper.
- Your Shorts and long videos stay on the same topics, so your channel feels consistent.

The important word is "reuse", not "copy". YouTube's Partner Program policies reject mass-produced, repetitive ("inauthentic") content, so a Short should be a tightened, re-edited version with its own hook, not a raw slice of the long video with nothing added.

## Start with clean chapters

Good Shorts start with good chapters. Before you plan anything, check that your long video's chapters follow YouTube's rules:

- The first timestamp is 0:00.
- There are at least three chapters.
- Each chapter is at least 10 seconds long.

Then check that each chapter title describes one idea in plain words. "Mistake 3: stirring the rice" is far easier to turn into a Short than "Part 3". If you write your script with section headings, as described in [how to write AI video script prompts that sound human](../ai-script-prompts-that-sound-human/), those headings can become your chapter titles directly.

## Step 1: Pick the chapters that can stand alone

Not every chapter works on its own. Go through your list and mark each one.

| Chapter type | Works as a Short? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A single tip or fact | Usually yes | Complete in under a minute |
| A common mistake | Usually yes | Clear problem and fix |
| A surprising answer | Usually yes | Built-in curiosity |
| Intro or recap | Rarely | Needs the rest of the video |
| A step that depends on earlier steps | Sometimes | Only if you add context |

Aim for three to five Shorts from a typical long video. Picking fewer, stronger ones is better than turning every chapter into a clip.

## Step 2: Write a new hook for each Short

A chapter in a long video can start gently because the viewer is already watching. A Short cannot. The first line has to tell the viewer what they are about to get.

Simple hook patterns that work from chapter content:

1. **State the mistake:** "Stop stirring your rice while it cooks."
2. **Ask the real question:** "Why is the night sky dark when the universe is full of light?"
3. **Give the result first:** "One rule makes a grocery budget much easier to stick to."
4. **Name the surprise:** "Cleopatra lived closer in time to the Moon landing than to the building of the Great Pyramid."

Only use a hook if the Short delivers on it. Keep hooks to one sentence and cut any warm-up words.

## Step 3: Add on-screen text

Many people watch Shorts with the sound low or off, and the first frame needs to make sense on its own. Plan one short line of on-screen text per Short:

- Keep it to roughly two to five words, the same discipline as [thumbnail text](../youtube-thumbnail-text-rules/).
- Make it add to the hook, not repeat it word for word.
- Place it where the Shorts interface won't cover it. The bottom and right edge carry buttons and captions, so keep text near the upper middle of the frame.

Example for the rice Short: hook "Stop stirring your rice while it cooks", on-screen text "GUMMY RICE FIX".

## Step 4: Title each Short

Shorts titles are seen in a busy feed, so they should be short and clear. A few rules:

- Lead with the specific idea, not the series name.
- Include your keyword if it fits naturally.
- Keep it honest; the clip is short, so the title should promise something small and exact.

Example Shorts titles from one long video about stargazing:

- Why Is the Night Sky Dark?
- Find Saturn in 30 Seconds With a Free App
- The Telescope Mistake Most Beginners Make

You can check drafts with the free [YouTube Title Scorer](../../tools/title-scorer/). Its 40–65 character range is aimed at long-video titles, so for Shorts treat a lower length score as fine and focus on the other checks: a strong start, no bait phrases and your keyword.

## Step 5: Edit and format

With the plan written, edit each Short:

1. Export the chapter section from your project, not from the final upload, so you can re-frame for vertical.
2. Re-frame to vertical (9:16). Move or crop visuals so the main subject stays centered.
3. Record or generate the new hook line and place it at the very start.
4. Trim pauses and anything that refers to "earlier in this video".
5. Keep it within YouTube's Shorts length limit; at the time of writing Shorts can be up to 3 minutes, but most single-idea clips need far less.
6. Add the on-screen text and captions.

If the clip uses a realistic AI voice or avatar, the same disclosure rule applies as for long videos: mark altered or synthetic realistic content in YouTube Studio.

## Step 6: Link back and schedule

Each Short should give interested viewers a path to the full video. At the time of writing, YouTube Studio lets you add a related video link to a Short; check the current options when you upload. Then spread the Shorts out instead of posting them all on the same day. A simple pattern is one Short a day or two after the long video, then the rest over the following week or two.
