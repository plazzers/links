A faceless channel can be a good home for a small digital product: a template, a checklist, a guide or a spreadsheet that helps viewers do what your videos teach. Payhip is one simple way to sell one. This guide covers the basics honestly: what to sell, how a free product helps, how to think about price, where to put links, and how coupons and affiliates work. It also says plainly what most new sellers find: products usually sell slowly at first, and there is no promise of sales.

## Start with what your viewers already ask for

The easiest product to sell is one your audience has already asked for, in comments or by the questions your videos answer. Look for something that saves your viewer time on a task your channel covers.

Good fits for a faceless channel:

- **Templates:** a budget spreadsheet, a meal planner, a study schedule.
- **Checklists and workbooks:** a printable version of the steps in a how-to video.
- **Short guides:** a focused PDF that goes deeper than a video can.
- **Resource packs:** organized lists, prompts or swipe files related to your niche.

Before you build anything, check three things:

1. Can you describe the product in one sentence that a viewer would recognize as their problem?
2. Is it clearly connected to what your channel already teaches?
3. Is it your own work? Do not resell content you do not have the rights to, and check the license of any fonts, images or templates you include.

## Set up a Payhip store

Payhip sells digital downloads and lets you start without a monthly fee. At the time of writing it has a free plan that takes a transaction fee on each sale, and paid plans with lower fees. Fees and features change, so check Payhip's pricing page for the current numbers before you decide.

A basic setup looks like this:

1. Create an account and set your store name to match your channel.
2. Add a product: upload the file, write a title and a description, add a cover image.
3. Connect a payout method as Payhip's settings describe.
4. Preview the product page on your phone, since many viewers will arrive from YouTube on mobile.
5. Buy your own product with a 100% coupon (see below) to test the download.

Write the product description plainly. Say what is inside, who it is for, the file format and what it does not include. Clear limits reduce refund requests and build trust.

## Offer a free product first

Payhip supports free products that collect an email address before the download. A free lead magnet is a small, genuinely useful item, such as a one-page checklist or a sample from your larger template.

Why it helps:

- Viewers can try your work without paying, which makes a later purchase easier.
- You build an email list you can reach directly, without depending on a recommendation algorithm.
- It shows you whether people want this kind of product at all before you build a bigger one.

Keep it short and finished. A free item that feels like a teaser with the useful part removed does more harm than good. If you collect emails, follow the rules for your region and only send what people signed up for.

## Think about price without guessing wildly

There is no correct price, and nobody can tell you what your product will earn. A few ways to think it through:

1. **Look at similar products** in your niche on Payhip and other marketplaces. Note what is included at each price, not just the price.
2. **Price the outcome, not the page count.** A short template that saves hours of setup can be worth more to a buyer than a long PDF.
3. **Start simple.** One product at one price is easier to test than a tiered bundle.
4. **Change one thing at a time.** If you adjust the price, leave the description and cover alone for a few weeks so you can tell what made a difference.

## Put links where viewers will look

Viewers will not find your product unless you point to it. Places to add the link:

- **The first lines of the video description**, with one sentence on what the product is. The top of the description is what shows before "more".
- **A pinned comment** on relevant videos.
- **A mention in the video** where the product naturally helps, for example "the checklist from this video is free, link in the description".
- **Your channel's links section** on the channel page.
- **Pinterest pins** that link to the product page or to the free lead magnet. Follow YouTube's rules for links and promotions, and disclose affiliate links or paid promotion where required.

## Use coupon codes on purpose

Payhip supports coupon codes. Use them with a reason, not constantly, or buyers learn to wait for a discount.

Sensible uses:

- A launch code for your email list or for people who downloaded the free product.
- A code mentioned in one specific video, so you can see whether that video sends buyers.
- A 100% code for testing your own checkout or for giving a review copy to a collaborator.

Give every code an expiry date and a name that tells you where it was shared, such as `SHORTS-OCT`.

## Consider an affiliate program

Payhip has an affiliate program where you set the commission. Other creators, often in a related niche, share your product with their own link and earn that commission on sales they send.

Before you open it:

- Make sure your product page is clear enough that someone else can recommend it without explaining it.
- Decide a commission you are comfortable paying on every sale.
- Ask affiliates to disclose their links, as platform rules and advertising laws generally require.

## Set honest expectations

Most new digital products sell slowly at first. Many sell nothing for a while, even when the product is good, because few people know it exists yet. There is no promise of sales, and this guide makes no income claims.

What tends to help over time is ordinary and steady: useful videos that relate to the product, links in consistent places, a free item that earns trust, and small improvements based on what you see in your weekly numbers. Our guide on [how to track what works](../weekly-content-review/) shows a simple way to keep an eye on that. Treat the product as part of your channel, not a replacement for making good videos.
