Pinterest works differently from most social platforms. People use it to search and save ideas for later, and pins can keep showing up in search long after you post them. That makes it a reasonable second channel for a YouTuber, especially a faceless one: your videos already answer questions, and Pinterest is a place where people go looking for answers. This guide covers how to set it up, what to pin for each video, where to link, and how to keep it to a routine you can sustain.

## Is Pinterest a good fit for your channel?

Pinterest suits some niches much better than others. Before you invest time, check whether your topics match what people search for there.

Good fits tend to be practical, visual or plannable topics:

- Recipes, meal prep and food
- Home, cleaning, organizing and DIY
- Personal finance, budgeting and saving tips
- Travel planning and destination guides
- Fitness routines, health habits and self-improvement
- Crafts, design, productivity and study tips

Weaker fits are topics that are mostly news, commentary, reactions or entertainment that only makes sense in video form.

A quick test: type three of your video topics into Pinterest search. If you see plenty of pins and search suggestions, people are looking for that topic there. If the results are thin or unrelated, Pinterest may be a slow channel for you.

## Set up your account and boards

1. **Create or convert to a business account.** It is free and gives you analytics and bulk create.
2. **Claim your website** if you have one, so your pins show your name and you can see analytics for it. You can also link your YouTube channel in your profile settings, if that option is available for your account.
3. **Write a clear profile.** Your display name and bio should say what your channel helps with, in plain words people would search for.
4. **Create 5 to 10 boards** that match your main video topics. Name them the way people search, for example "Budget Meal Prep" rather than "My Kitchen Stuff".
5. **Add a short description to each board** with the main keywords for that topic.

Boards must exist before you can bulk upload into them, so set these up first.

## What to pin for each video

One video can produce several different pins. Each one takes a different angle, links back to the same video, and gives Pinterest more ways to show your content in search.

| Pin type | Example for a meal prep video | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Headline pin | "7 Budget Meal Prep Ideas for Busy Weeks" | The main search phrase |
| Numbered list | "5 Containers That Make Meal Prep Easier" | Saving for later |
| Quick tip | "Cook Rice Once for Three Different Meals" | One clear takeaway |
| Checklist | "Sunday Meal Prep Checklist" | Practical, printable-style value |

Tips for the images:

- Use the 2:3 pin size, 1000×1500 pixels.
- Put a short, readable headline on the image. Many people see the pin small on a phone.
- Keep your colors and fonts consistent so pins look like they come from the same creator.
- Do not reuse your YouTube thumbnail as-is. A 1280×720 thumbnail is the wrong shape and gets small in the feed.

Write the pin title and description for search. Describe what the person gets from the video in the words they would type, and avoid clickbait that the video does not deliver.

## Where to link: video, website or product

Every pin has a link. You have three main options:

1. **Directly to the YouTube video.** Simple, and the viewer lands on the content the pin promised. This is a good default.
2. **To a blog post or page on your site that embeds the video.** More work, but you own the page, can add a written summary, and can link to related videos or a free download.
3. **To a product or free resource.** If you sell a digital product, a checklist pin can link to a free version that collects an email, or to the product page. The basics are in [selling digital products from a faceless channel](../sell-digital-products-faceless-channel/).

Whichever you choose, add UTM parameters to links that go to your own website, for example `?utm_source=pinterest&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=meal-prep`. Then you can see in your analytics which pins and topics send visitors. Do not rely on UTM tags for youtube.com links; use YouTube Studio's traffic sources report to see how much traffic comes from Pinterest instead.

Make sure the link matches the pin. A pin about rice should not send people to a general channel page.

## Make pins in batches, not one by one

Making pins every time you publish a video is easy to fall behind on. Batching works better:

1. **Once a week**, list the videos you want to promote (new ones and older ones that still fit).
2. **Write 3 to 5 pin ideas per video** in a spreadsheet: title, headline text, description, board and link.
3. **Create the images** from a few reusable templates.
4. **Host the images** so each one has a public link. A free way is described in [hosting Pinterest images with GitHub Pages](../host-pinterest-images-github-pages/).
5. **Upload them all at once** with a CSV. The step-by-step process and the exact header line are in [how to bulk upload pins with a CSV](../pinterest-bulk-upload-csv/).

## Schedule pins steadily

Pinterest's own advice to businesses has generally been to pin consistently rather than post everything at once and then go quiet. You do not need to pin constantly, but a steady rhythm is easier to keep up and easier to measure.

A simple approach:

- Spread each batch over the coming week or two using the Publish date column.
- Pin a few times a day rather than dozens at once.
- Space out pins for the same video by a few days, so similar pins do not appear back to back.
- Mix new videos with older ones that are still useful.

Pinterest's own recommendations on pin frequency change over time, so check its business help pages for current guidance rather than following a fixed number.

## Measure what works

After a few weeks you can see patterns. Once a week, check:

- **Pinterest analytics:** which pins get saves and outbound clicks, and which boards perform.
- **YouTube Studio:** traffic sources, to see whether Pinterest sends views and to which videos.
- **Your website analytics:** visits tagged with `utm_source=pinterest`, if you link to your own pages.

Write down two or three observations, such as "checklist pins get more saves" or "finance pins get clicks, recipe pins get saves only". Then make more of what works. A simple routine for this is in [how to track what works with a weekly review](../weekly-content-review/).

Be patient with it. Pinterest is often a slow-building channel, and there is no promise of views or sales from any particular number of pins. Treat it as a steady habit alongside your videos.

## A weekly Pinterest routine for YouTubers

Put it together and the whole thing fits into one session a week:

1. Pick the videos to promote this week.
2. Write pin ideas in your spreadsheet.
3. Make the images from templates.
4. Upload images, build the CSV, check it, and bulk upload.
5. Review last week's numbers and adjust your pin ideas.

Start small, with a handful of videos and a few pins each. Once the routine feels easy, add more boards and older videos.
