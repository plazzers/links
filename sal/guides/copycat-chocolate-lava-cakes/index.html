Copycat Chocolate Lava Cakes: The Restaurant Dessert at Home

Copycat chocolate lava cakes like the casual dining chains: why the middle stays molten, key tips and mistakes. About 25 minutes, roughly $1.25 a cake.

You know the moment. The server sets it down: a little dark chocolate cake on a white plate, a scoop of vanilla ice cream melting next to it, maybe a dusting of powdered sugar. You press your spoon in and warm chocolate comes running out of the middle. The whole table goes quiet.

Then the bill comes, and that little cake costs about as much as a whole box of good chocolate.

Here's the good news: the molten chocolate cake is one of the easiest restaurant desserts there is. A handful of pantry ingredients, a few ramekins, about 25 minutes, and roughly $1.25 a cake. And for holiday dinners it's perfect, because you can do almost all the work in the morning.

Why the restaurant version works

A lava cake is not a magic trick. It's a timing trick.

The batter is rich with chocolate, butter and eggs, and only a little flour. In a hot oven, the outside sets into a tender cake while the center is still soft and saucy. Take it out at the right moment and you get both: a cake on the outside, warm chocolate in the middle.

That's why restaurants love it:

  • It's made ahead. The ramekins are filled earlier in the day and kept in the fridge. When you order, they go straight into the oven.
  • It bakes fast. A few minutes in the oven while your table finishes dinner.
  • It looks like a lot of work. It isn't.

You can do exactly the same thing at home.

Sal's key tips

Use chocolate you'd eat

The chocolate is the whole show. Use a semi-sweet bar or good chips you'd happily eat on their own. Chop it so it melts evenly, and melt it gently with the butter, low heat or short bursts in the microwave, stirring often. Chocolate that gets too hot turns grainy and stiff.

Prepare the ramekins properly

Butter the inside of each ramekin well, every corner, then dust with cocoa powder and tap out the extra. That's what lets the cake slide out clean when you flip it. Flour works too, but cocoa doesn't leave a white coat on a dark cake.

Don't overmix

Once the flour goes in, fold just until you can't see it anymore. Beating the batter hard makes the cake tougher and can make it rise and crack.

Fill ahead, bake to order

Here's the restaurant move. Fill the ramekins in the morning, cover them and put them in the fridge. Bake them while everyone's clearing the dinner plates. Straight from the fridge they need a couple of extra minutes, so watch them.

Learn your oven

Every oven is a little different. The first time, bake one test cake. The edges should look set and firm, and the center should still look soft and jiggle a little when you nudge the ramekin. Note the time. That's your number from now on.

The flip

Let the cakes sit for a minute or so out of the oven. Run a thin knife around the edge, put a plate on top, and flip with confidence. Use oven mitts: the ramekin is very hot. Lift it straight up.

The ingredients (names only)

By name only. Exact amounts and the full step-by-step are in Sal's Restaurant Copycat Cookbook and in Sal's Kitchen App.

  • Semi-sweet chocolate, chopped
  • Butter
  • Powdered sugar
  • Eggs and extra egg yolks, for richness
  • Vanilla
  • Flour, just a little
  • Butter and cocoa powder for the ramekins
  • Vanilla ice cream to serve

That's it. No special mix, no secret ingredient. The restaurant has the same list.

The method, the restaurant way

No measurements here, but here's the rhythm:

  1. Prepare the ramekins with butter and cocoa.
  2. Melt the chocolate and butter together gently, then let it cool a little.
  3. Whisk in the powdered sugar, then the eggs and yolks, then the vanilla.
  4. Fold in the flour, just until it disappears.
  5. Fill the ramekins. Bake now, or cover and refrigerate for later.
  6. Bake in a hot oven until the edges are set and the center still jiggles.
  7. Rest, loosen and flip onto plates. Serve right away with ice cream.

Common mistakes

  • Overbaking. The most common one by far. You get a nice chocolate cake, but no lava. Pull them while the center still looks soft.
  • Underbaking. The opposite problem: the cake collapses when you flip it. If that happens, call it "chocolate pudding cake," serve it in the ramekin, and bake the next one a bit longer.
  • Skipping the cocoa dusting. The cake sticks and tears.
  • Overheating the chocolate. Melt slowly and keep stirring.
  • Waiting too long to flip. A few minutes on the counter and the middle keeps cooking from the heat of the ramekin. A minute is plenty.
  • Using different-sized ramekins in the same batch. They bake at different speeds. Use matching ones.

About the soft center and eggs

A lava cake's center is meant to be soft, which means the eggs in the middle aren't fully cooked the way they would be in a regular cake. FoodSafety.gov lists 160°F as the safe minimum temperature for egg dishes. If you're serving young children, older guests, someone who's pregnant or anyone who prefers to play it safe, use pasteurized eggs, which are sold in many grocery stores, or simply bake the cakes a few minutes longer until they're set all the way through. They'll still be rich, chocolatey and delicious, just more like a warm fudge cake.

How to serve it

  • Vanilla ice cream is the classic. The cold against the warm is half the fun.
  • Powdered sugar through a small strainer on top, right before serving.
  • Fresh raspberries or a few strawberries on the side for color.
  • Whipped cream if ice cream isn't your thing.
  • For a holiday table, a sprig of mint and a few red berries on each plate looks like a restaurant dessert, because it is one.

Lava cakes are best fresh out of the oven. Leftover baked cakes can go in the fridge and be warmed briefly in the microwave. The middle won't flow like the first night, but nobody has ever complained.

What it costs at home

About 25 minutes, roughly $1.25 a cake.

Compare that with the dessert line on a restaurant bill, and with a little tip on top. For a family of four, that's a restaurant-quality dessert for about the price of one fancy coffee. Bottom line

  • Good chocolate, melted gently.
  • Butter and cocoa in every ramekin.
  • Fold the flour in gently. Don't beat it.
  • Fill ahead, bake to order, pull them while the middle still jiggles.
  • Rest a minute, flip with confidence, serve with ice cream.
  • Pasteurized eggs or a longer bake if anyone at the table should skip the soft center.
