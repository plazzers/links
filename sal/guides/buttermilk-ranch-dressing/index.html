Homemade Buttermilk Ranch That Beats the Bottle

Homemade buttermilk ranch like the wing and pizza places serve: fresh herbs, tangy buttermilk, 10 minutes plus a chill. About $1.50 a batch. Free recipe.

The ranch at the wing and pizza places is thicker, tangier and fresher than anything in a bottle. People dip everything in it. People ask for extra cups and pay for them. And it's one of the easiest things you can make in your kitchen.

Mayonnaise, sour cream, buttermilk, fresh herbs, a little garlic and onion powder, lemon, salt and pepper. Whisk, chill, done. About 10 minutes of work plus an hour in the fridge, and roughly $1.50 a batch.

The full recipe card is right below, free from Sal's Restaurant Copycat Cookbook. Around it, I'll explain why fresh beats the bottle, how to make it thick for dipping or thin for salad, and a few ways to change it up.

Why homemade ranch tastes so much better

Here's my honest opinion: the bottle never tastes like this. Shelf-stable dressing has to last for months, so it's built for the shelf, not for flavor. A fresh batch tastes bright and herby, with a real buttermilk tang.

What makes the restaurant-style version work:

  • Three creamy bases together. Mayo for body, sour cream for richness and tang, buttermilk for that classic ranch flavor and to loosen it up.
  • Fresh herbs. Dill, chives and parsley. This is the difference.
  • A little allium. Garlic powder and onion powder give that savory ranch flavor everybody recognizes. Here, powder beats fresh, because it spreads evenly and doesn't get harsh.
  • Acid. A bit of lemon juice keeps it lively.
  • Time. The chill isn't optional. The herbs and powders need time to wake up.

Buttermilk Ranch: the full recipe

Makes
about 1 1/2 cups
Time
10 min + 1 hr chill
Cost
about $1.50

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup mayonnaise
  • 1/2 cup sour cream
  • 1/2 cup buttermilk
  • 1 tbsp fresh dill (or 1 tsp dried)
  • 1 tbsp fresh chives
  • 1 tbsp fresh parsley
  • 1/2 tsp garlic powder, 1/2 tsp onion powder
  • 1 tsp lemon juice, salt and pepper

Method

  1. Whisk everything together.
  2. Thin with more buttermilk if you want it for salad, keep it thick for dipping.
  3. Chill at least 1 hour. Keeps 5 days.

Sal's tip: Fresh herbs if you can. The bottle never tastes like this.

Technique: small things that make a big difference

Fresh herbs if you can

This is the rule. Fresh herbs, if you can get them. Dried herbs work in a pinch, but fresh dill and chives are what make people ask, "What did you put in this?" Chop them fine so every spoonful has some.

Whisk until smooth

Start with the mayo and sour cream, whisk them smooth, then add the buttermilk a little at a time. If you dump it all in at once, you can end up chasing lumps.

Chill before serving

Right after mixing, ranch tastes thin and the garlic and onion powders taste a little raw. After an hour in the fridge, it thickens and the flavors come together. Make it ahead; it's even better the next day.

Thick for dipping, thin for salad

Want a dip for wings, fries or vegetables? Keep it thick. Want a salad dressing that pours? Whisk in a little more buttermilk until it's the way you like it. Same recipe, two jobs.

Taste and adjust at the end

Taste after it's chilled, not before. Cold food tastes less salty, so season for how it will be served. More lemon if it's flat, a pinch more salt if it's dull, more pepper if you like a kick.

Common mistakes

  1. Serving it right away. It tastes thin and a little raw. Give it the chill.
  2. Dried herbs only, and too many of them. Dried dill is strong. If you must use dried, go lighter than you think.
  3. Fresh garlic, raw. It gets sharp and takes over as it sits. Powder is smoother here.
  4. Seasoning before chilling. Taste it cold, then adjust.
  5. Watery ranch. Too much buttermilk too fast. Add it a little at a time until it's the texture you want.

Variations

  • Spicy ranch. A spoonful of hot sauce or a pinch of cayenne. Perfect next to wings.
  • Smoky ranch. Blend in a little smoked chili in adobo sauce, or a pinch of smoked paprika, for smoky heat.
  • Avocado ranch. Blend in a ripe avocado for a thick, green dip.
  • Lighter ranch. Swap some of the mayo and sour cream for plain Greek yogurt.
  • Extra-herby. Add fresh basil or tarragon with the other herbs.
  • Dry mix. If you don't keep buttermilk around, you can make a ranch-style dip with sour cream and herbs. It won't have the same tang, though, so I'd buy the buttermilk.

Leftover buttermilk? Use it up!

People buy a carton of buttermilk for one recipe and the rest sits there. Don't let it. Use it in pancakes or biscuits, or as a marinade for fried chicken; it makes the meat tender and helps the coating stick. Or just make more ranch. Nobody ever complained about extra ranch.

Serving ideas

  • Wings. The classic, especially with a spicy or buffalo-style sauce.
  • Pizza. Yes, people dip pizza in ranch. An Italian cook is supposed to complain. I won't. Try it with a crust.
  • Raw vegetables. Carrots, celery, cucumbers, peppers, cherry tomatoes. A party platter that actually gets eaten.
  • Salads. Thinned out over a crunchy wedge or a chopped salad with bacon and tomatoes.
  • Fries and potatoes. Dip, drizzle, or spoon over a baked potato.
  • Burgers and wraps. A swipe on a burger bun or inside a chicken wrap. If you're doing burger night, try my smash burgers with special sauce and put the ranch out too.
  • Chips and dip with salsa. Next to a bowl of restaurant-style blender salsa, for the people who can't choose.

Storing and cost

Storing it

Keep it in a covered jar in the fridge. Stir or shake before serving, because it may thicken as it sits; a splash of buttermilk loosens it again. The recipe card above tells you how long it keeps.

Is it worth making instead of buying?

At about $1.50 a batch, it's often cheaper than a bottle of the good stuff, and it tastes fresher. And at a restaurant, that little cup of extra ranch can come with an extra charge. The short version

  • Mayo, sour cream and buttermilk, whisked smooth.
  • Fresh herbs if you can. The bottle never tastes like this.
  • Chill it at least an hour before serving.
  • Thick for dipping, thinned for salad.
