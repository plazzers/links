# SPEC v4 — Guide sites (SEO content hubs) for Walter and Sal

Read README.md, the existing pages/tools, and everything in `content-sources/` first.

Goal: two real content websites inside this repo that bring search (Google) and Pinterest traffic, and turn readers into email subscribers (free PDFs) and buyers (apps, books). They sit next to the existing link pages and tools:
- `https://plazzers.github.io/links/walter/guides/` (index) and `/walter/guides/<slug>/`
- `https://plazzers.github.io/links/sal/guides/` and `/sal/guides/<slug>/`
The existing link pages keep working; add a clear "Guides" button to them.

## 1. Tech
- Static HTML generated by a small Python build script (`tools/build_guides.py`) from Markdown + YAML front matter in `content/walter/*.md` and `content/sal/*.md` (use only stdlib + `markdown` + `pyyaml` — vendor nothing at runtime; the generated HTML is committed). No JS frameworks; tiny vanilla JS only for the table of contents highlight and share button.
- Each article: semantic HTML, `<title>` ≤ 60 chars, meta description ≤ 155, canonical URL, Open Graph + Twitter tags with a generated og image (reuse/extend `tools/make_og.py`), JSON-LD (`Article`; for Sal recipe-style articles use `Article`, NOT `Recipe` schema since we don't publish full recipes), breadcrumb, reading time, table of contents, "Last updated" date, related articles (3), and author box (see §4).
- `sitemap.xml` (for the whole `links` site) and `robots.txt` at the repo root, plus `feed.xml` (RSS) per channel.
- Fast: no external fonts (system stacks), images WebP/JPEG lazy-loaded, Lighthouse-style sanity (no layout shift, good contrast).
- Brand look per channel, same as the link pages (Walter navy/orange blueprint; Sal espresso/cream/tomato with tri-stripe).
- Every Payhip/product link: `?utm_source=guides&utm_medium=article&utm_campaign=<slug>`.

## 2. Walter content (30 articles, 900–1,600 words each)
Source material: `content-sources/walter/checklist-content.md` (the app's checklist with tips), `content-sources/walter/maintenance-rules.js`, the house-age tool content, and these published video topics: "Never Buy a House If You See These 7 Things", "9 Things to Check Before Winter", "Do These 7 Things Before Bed", "If Your House Was Built Before 1990, Check These 7 Things", "7 Small House Problems That Get Expensive Fast".
Write original, genuinely useful, accurate, conservative articles in Walter's calm plain voice. Suggested set (adjust for search intent; one primary keyword each, listed in front matter):
- Buying: house red flags at a showing; foundation crack types (what to worry about); signs of water in a basement; how to spot a bad flip; questions to ask the seller; what a home inspection does and doesn't cover; sewer line camera inspection explained; buying an older home checklist.
- Seasonal: winterize your house checklist; before the first freeze; spring home maintenance checklist; fall gutter & drainage guide; summer storm prep.
- Systems: how long a roof lasts & signs it's near the end; water heater age & warning signs; furnace warning signs; sump pump testing; GFCI outlets explained; smoke & CO alarm guide; dryer vent fire risk; attic ventilation basics; radon basics.

Rules: educational only; clear "look yourself" vs "call a licensed pro" lines; no repair prices; cite authoritative public sources at the end of each article where facts are specific (EPA, CPSC, NFPA, US Fire Administration, energy.gov, InterNACHI/ASHI public pages) with links; no fabricated statistics. Each article embeds: a box for the FREE Weekend Home Check (https://payhip.com/b/hiIm1) near the top or middle, a contextual product box near the end (App https://payhip.com/b/OZeda for checklist/maintenance topics; Red Flag Checklist https://payhip.com/b/HAfRF for buying topics; Manual https://payhip.com/b/ABaxT for room-by-room/seasonal topics), a link to the house-age tool when relevant, and "Watch on YouTube" (https://www.youtube.com/@WaltersHomeCheck).

## 4. Author box & About pages — honesty rule (important)
Write in Walter's / Sal's voice and brand, but do NOT state invented credentials or biography as fact anywhere on these sites (no "licensed inspector", "40 years as a home inspector", "Michelin-starred chef", "ran restaurants in Milan", etc.). Author box text should be brand-level and true, e.g.:
- Walter: "Walter's Home Check — plain-English home checks from the YouTube channel. We explain what to look at and when to call a licensed pro."
- Sal: "Chef Sal Romano — restaurant secrets and copycat recipes from the YouTube channel, made for home cooks."
Each channel gets an `about/` page describing the channel, what the content is (educational guides and recipes), how products work, contact email (waltershomecheck@outlook.com / salromanochef@outlook.com), and the disclaimers. No fake reviews, testimonials or ratings anywhere.
