Spring Home Maintenance Checklist: What to Check After Winter

A practical spring home maintenance checklist: roof, gutters, foundation, sump pump, faucets and AC — what to check yourself and when to call a pro.

Spring is when a house tells you what winter did to it. Ice, wind, freeze-thaw cycles and months of closed windows all leave marks, and most of them are easy to spot if you know where to look.

A good spring home maintenance checklist has one main job: find water damage early and get water moving away from the house before the heavy rains. After that, it's about getting the cooling system ready and catching small problems while they're still small.

You can do most of this in a weekend. Here's how I'd work through it, outside first, then in.

Walk the outside of the house

Start with a slow lap around the house on a dry day. Bring a notepad or your phone for photos.

  • Ground slope. Soil and mulch should slope away from the foundation on every side. Winter can settle soil and create low spots that send rain straight toward the basement.
  • Foundation. Look for new cracks. Hairline cracks are common. Wide cracks, horizontal cracks, stair-step cracks in block or brick, or cracks that have grown since last year are worth having a structural engineer look at. The foundation crack types guide explains the difference.
  • Siding and trim. Check the bottom edges and around windows for peeling paint, soft wood or gaps. That's where rot starts.
  • Walks and steps. Frost can heave slabs and loosen pavers. Note any new trip hazards.
  • Pest signs. Thin mud tubes running up the foundation can mean termites. That's a job for a pest control company.

Roof and gutters (from the ground)

Winter is hard on roofs. Look from the yard with binoculars:

  • Missing, cracked, curled or lifted shingles.
  • Flashing around the chimney, vents and skylights that looks loose or bent.
  • Heavy granule loss — lots of sand-like grit in the gutters or at the downspout outlets.
  • Sagging lines along the ridge or roof edges.

Don't climb up to check. If something looks off, call a roofer.

Then clean the gutters and check every downspout. Water should exit several feet from the house — an extension piece is a simple fix. If your roof is high or steep, hire a gutter service rather than climbing. The fall gutter and drainage guide has ladder safety tips that apply in spring too.

Turn the outside water back on

If you shut off and drained your outdoor faucets in the fall, this is when you find out whether a pipe split over winter.

  1. Open the indoor shut-off valve for each outdoor faucet.
  2. Turn on the outside faucet and let it run.
  3. Go back inside and look at the wall behind it. Check the basement ceiling or crawlspace for drips or wet spots.
  4. Reconnect the hose and watch the connection for leaks.

A frozen pipe that cracked in January often shows up right now. If you see water inside, shut the valve again and call a plumber.

Basement, crawlspace and sump pump

Spring rain and snowmelt test your basement. After the first heavy rain, go down with a flashlight.

  • Look for water in corners and where the floor meets the wall.
  • Check for white chalky deposits on the walls. That's mineral residue left behind by water coming through.
  • Use your nose. A musty smell means moisture somewhere. The EPA's guidance on mold is simple: the key to mold control is moisture control, so find and fix the water source.
  • Test the sump pump. Slowly pour a bucket of water into the pit. The pump should switch on and empty it by itself. Make sure the discharge pipe outside is clear. Our sump pump testing guide walks through it.
  • Crawlspace: look in from the hatch with a good flashlight. The plastic ground cover should be in place, with no standing water. Don't crawl in if you see wires hanging down or signs of animals — call a pro.

If you find water, call a waterproofing contractor. If you see mold covering a large area, the EPA suggests getting professional help rather than cleaning it yourself.

Get the cooling system ready

Better to find an AC problem in spring than on the first hot day, when everyone is calling.

  • Clear around the outdoor unit. Pull leaves and weeds and trim back plants so it has open space on all sides. Leave the panels on.
  • Check or change the filter. Energy.gov notes that a dirty filter reduces airflow and makes the system work harder.
  • Book a yearly AC tune-up with an HVAC technician. They handle the refrigerant, electrical parts and coils.
  • Don't run the AC to test it while it's still cold outside — that can damage some systems. Wait for a warm day.

Inside the house

A quick pass through each room:

  • Ceilings: look for new water stains, especially under bathrooms and along outside walls.
  • Under sinks: open every cabinet with a flashlight. Feel the cabinet floor for soft or warped wood. Spring is a good time to check for slow leaks — the EPA's WaterSense program runs Fix a Leak Week each March for exactly this reason.
  • Toilets: a few drops of food coloring in the tank. If color shows up in the bowl without flushing, the flapper is leaking.
  • Washing machine hoses: look for bulges or cracks. Replace them if you see either.
  • Windows and screens: check screens for tears and clean out window tracks so water can drain.

Safety checks

  • Test every smoke and CO alarm. Change batteries if you do that when the clocks change in March.
  • Test GFCI outlets in the kitchen, bathrooms, garage and outside. Press TEST, then RESET. If one won't trip or reset, call an electrician. More in GFCI outlets explained.
  • Garage door: lay a roll of paper towels where the door closes. It should touch it and reverse. If it doesn't, call a garage door company. Never adjust the springs yourself.
  • Deck and railings: give every railing a firm shake and look underneath where the deck meets the house. If anything moves, stay off it and call a carpenter or deck builder.

The short version

  • Walk the outside: slope, foundation, siding, and roof from the ground.
  • Clean the gutters and make sure downspouts carry water away.
  • Turn outdoor faucets back on and check inside for leaks.
  • Check the basement after rain and test the sump pump.
  • Get the AC serviced, and test your alarms and GFCIs.
