Christmas Tree and Holiday Lights Safety: A Simple Checklist

Christmas tree safety made simple: water, distance from heat, checking light strings, extension cords, outdoor lights, candles and when the tree goes out.

The tree goes up, the boxes come down from the attic, and for a few weeks the house has more lights, cords and candles in it than the rest of the year combined. That's a big part of what makes December feel like December.

It's also a time of year when fire safety groups like NFPA and the U.S. Fire Administration remind people to slow down and check a few things. None of it is complicated. Most of it takes a few minutes. Here's a simple checklist for the tree, the lights, the cords and the candles, from the day you bring the tree home to the day it goes out the door.

Picking and setting up the tree

If you buy a real tree

  • Pick a fresh one. Fresh needles are green, bend instead of snapping, and don't fall off in a shower when you tap the trunk on the ground.
  • Cut a little off the bottom of the trunk before it goes in the stand, or ask the seller to do it. A fresh cut helps the tree take up water.
  • Use a stand that holds plenty of water, and fill it right away.

If you use an artificial tree

  • Look for one labeled as fire resistant on the box or tag.
  • Check the built-in lights on pre-lit trees the same way you'd check any light string (more on that below).

Where the tree goes

This is the big one. Keep the tree at least 3 feet away from anything that makes heat: fireplaces, wood stoves, space heaters, heat vents, radiators and candles. Our space heater safety guide uses the same 3-foot rule for the same reason.

Also make sure the tree doesn't block a doorway, a hallway or a window someone might need to get out through. A tree in front of the only exit from a room is a problem even if it never catches fire.

Water it every day

A real tree drinks a lot, especially in the first week. A tree that runs out of water dries out, and a dry tree burns very quickly.

  • Check the water level every day and keep it above the cut on the trunk.
  • Make it part of a routine, like the morning coffee, so it doesn't get forgotten during busy weeks.
  • Watch for drying. Needles that fall when you brush past, or branches that snap instead of bend, mean the tree is drying out.

Check the light strings before they go up

Most of us pull the lights out of the box, plug them in, and if they light up, on they go. Take five more minutes and look at them first, ideally on a table in good light.

  1. Look for a safety label. Lights should carry the mark of a recognized testing laboratory. Use indoor lights indoors and lights marked for outdoor use outside.
  2. Check the cords. Cracked, frayed or pinched wires, bare copper, or a cord that's been chewed by a pet means that string is done. Don't tape it up.
  3. Check the plugs and sockets. Broken or loose sockets, scorch marks, melted plastic or a plug that gets warm are all reasons to replace the string.
  4. Read the box or tag for how many strings can be connected end to end, and stick to it.
  5. Throw out damaged strings. Light strings are cheap compared to what they can cause.

Hanging them

  • Don't use nails, tacks or staples through or around the wires. Plastic clips made for holiday lights hold them without damaging the cord.
  • Keep bulbs from resting on paper, fabric or dry branches near the trunk.
  • Use a sturdy ladder for the high spots, set it on firm, level ground, and have someone hold it. Stay away from power lines outside.

Extension cords and outlets

Holiday lighting often means extension cords, power strips and more things plugged into one outlet than usual.

  • Use the right cord for the job. Outdoor lights need an extension cord rated for outdoor use.
  • Don't run cords under rugs or carpets, or pinch them in doors and windows. Walking on them wears the insulation down where you can't see it.
  • Don't overload outlets or chain power strips together. If plugs or cords feel warm, unplug them and spread things out.
  • Outdoors, plug into a GFCI-protected outlet. GFCI outlets cut the power if something goes wrong with water around. Press TEST and RESET first to be sure it works. Our GFCI outlets explained guide shows how.
  • Keep connections off the ground outside, so they don't sit in snow or puddles.

If breakers trip, outlets spark, or a cord or plug gets hot or smells like burning plastic, unplug what you can and call a licensed electrician.

Turn them off at night

This is the easiest habit on the list. Turn off tree lights and decorations when you go to bed or leave the house. A timer or a smart plug makes it automatic. It's the holiday version of the routine in our nightly home safety routine guide.

Candles

Candles are lovely and they're also an open flame in a house full of paper, fabric and greenery.

  • Keep candles at least 12 inches from anything that can burn: the tree, wreaths, curtains, wrapping paper, cards.
  • Use sturdy holders that won't tip, on a flat surface out of reach of kids and pets.
  • Blow them out when you leave the room or go to bed.
  • Consider battery candles for windows, the mantel and anywhere near the tree. They look surprisingly real.

Fireplaces and wrapping paper

If you light a fire on a cold evening, keep stockings, garlands and gift piles well away from the opening, and use a screen. Don't burn wrapping paper in the fireplace. It can flare up fast and send burning bits up the chimney. Bag it for the trash or recycling instead. The fireplace safety checklist covers the rest.

Smoke alarms: the backup plan

All of this is about preventing a fire. Smoke alarms are what warn you if one starts anyway.

  • Press the test button on every alarm before the holidays. NFPA recommends testing smoke alarms monthly.
  • Check the date on the back. NFPA says smoke alarms should be replaced when they're 10 years old.
  • Make sure there's one on every level and outside sleeping areas, and inside bedrooms.
  • Talk about two ways out of every room with the family, especially with guests staying over.

Our smoke and CO alarm guide has more on placement and testing.

When the tree comes down

  • Take a real tree out once it's dry, or soon after the holidays, whichever comes first. A dry tree in the house is the riskiest stage.
  • Don't store it in the garage or lean it against the house while you wait for pickup. Check how your town collects trees; many recycle them.
  • Pack the lights carefully. Wrap strings loosely around a piece of cardboard so the wires don't kink, and toss any that came down damaged.

Your holiday checklist

  • Fresh tree, fresh cut, stand full of water
  • Tree at least 3 feet from heat sources, not blocking an exit
  • Water checked every day
  • Light strings checked for damage and labels before hanging
  • Outdoor lights and cords rated for outdoor use, plugged into a GFCI
  • No cords under rugs, no overloaded outlets
  • Lights off at bedtime and when you leave
  • Candles 12 inches from anything that burns, or battery candles
  • Smoke alarms tested
  • Tree out of the house once it's dry

The short version

Keep the tree watered and away from heat, check the lights before they go up, use the right cords, turn everything off at bedtime and keep candles well clear of anything that burns. Test your smoke alarms while you're at it. Then enjoy the lights. That's what they're for.
