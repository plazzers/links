Nightly Home Safety Checklist: 7 Things to Do Before Bed

A simple nightly home safety checklist: 7 things to do before bed, from the stove and dryer to doors, chargers and space heaters. Takes about two minutes.

The few minutes before bed are the best time to make your house safer, because the next several hours are when everyone is asleep and slowest to notice a problem. A nightly home safety checklist doesn't need to be long. Seven quick checks, about two minutes, the same way every night.

This isn't about worrying. It's a habit, like brushing your teeth. Once you've done it for a couple of weeks, you'll do it without thinking — and you'll sleep a little easier knowing the stove is off and the doors are closed.

Here are the seven things I'd do before bed, and why each one matters.

1. Check the stove, oven and small appliances

Cooking is a leading cause of home fires, according to the U.S. Fire Administration. Before bed, glance at every knob:

  • Stove burners off. Look at the knobs, don't just trust that you turned them off.
  • Oven off.
  • Toaster oven, coffee maker, slow cooker, air fryer — off, and ideally unplugged if they don't need to stay on.
  • Nothing on or against the stovetop — dish towels, paper, pot holders.

If you have a gas stove and ever smell gas, don't turn anything on or off. Leave the house and call the gas company from outside.

2. Turn off space heaters and blow out candles

Space heaters and candles are both easy to forget when you're tired.

  • Turn off and unplug space heaters before you go to sleep. NFPA's heating safety guidance says to turn them off when you leave the room or go to bed, and to keep anything that can burn at least three feet away.
  • Blow out every candle. Walk through the rooms you used tonight and check.
  • Fireplace: let the fire burn down, and use a sturdy screen in front of it. Never close the damper while there are still hot embers — that can push carbon monoxide into the house.

3. Don't run the dryer or dishwasher overnight

It's tempting to start a load and go to bed. I'd rather not.

  • Dryer: clean the lint filter before or after every load. NFPA notes that failing to clean dryers is a leading factor in dryer fires. Run it when you're awake and home.
  • Dishwasher and washing machine: a hose or valve that lets go at 2 a.m. can run for hours before anyone notices. Run them while you're up.

If your dryer takes longer than it used to, the vent duct may be clogged with lint. The dryer vent fire risk guide explains what to check and when to call a dryer vent cleaning service.

4. Lock the doors and close the garage

  • Lock every exterior door, including the one from the garage into the house.
  • Close the garage door. It's easy to leave it open overnight without noticing.
  • Check that the door between the garage and the house is closed. It's there to slow a fire and keep car exhaust out.
  • Turn on an outside light or make sure your motion lights work.

Keep keys somewhere easy to grab in a hurry. You want locks that keep people out but let you get out fast.

5. Close bedroom doors

This one surprises people. Sleeping with the bedroom door closed can slow the spread of smoke and heat if a fire starts elsewhere in the house. Fire safety groups have promoted this for years with the simple slogan "close before you doze."

If a closed door makes it hard to hear a child or someone who needs help, use a baby monitor or make sure you have interconnected smoke alarms so an alarm in one room sounds in all of them.

6. Unplug or check chargers and cords

  • Don't charge phones, tablets or laptops under pillows or on the bed. They need air to stay cool.
  • E-bike, scooter and tool batteries: charge them while you're awake, on a hard surface, and unplug them when they're full. Use the charger that came with them.
  • Extension cords and power strips shouldn't run under rugs or be daisy-chained together. If one feels warm, unplug it.
  • Outlets or switches that feel warm aren't normal. Stop using them and call a licensed electrician.

7. Know your way out

The last check is the one you hope you never need.

  • Clear the path from your bedroom to the exits. No laundry baskets, toys or bags to trip over in the dark.
  • Keep a flashlight and your phone by the bed.
  • Make sure bedroom windows open and aren't blocked by furniture. Every bedroom should have two ways out.
  • Know the sound of your smoke alarm and where everyone meets outside.

You can't check smoke alarms every night, but you can test them once a month as part of the monthly house walk. NFPA recommends monthly testing and replacing smoke alarms every 10 years. Our smoke and CO alarm guide covers placement and testing.

Making the routine stick

The easiest way to build this habit is to do it in the same order every night, along the same path through the house:

  1. Kitchen — stove, oven, small appliances.
  2. Living room — space heaters, candles, fireplace, chargers.
  3. Laundry — dryer and washer off.
  4. Doors — front, back, garage.
  5. Hallway — clear path.
  6. Bedroom — door closed, phone and flashlight nearby.

If you live with other people, split it up. One person does the kitchen and doors, another does the rest. Kids can own a step or two — checking the candles or the back door — which also teaches them the habit.

When to call a pro

The nightly routine is about habits, not repairs. But if you notice any of these during your check, don't wait:

  • Warm outlets, buzzing switches or flickering lights — a licensed electrician.
  • A gas smell — leave and call the gas company from outside.
  • A CO alarm going off — get everyone outside and call 911.
  • A dryer that's slow or very hot — a dryer vent cleaning service.

The short version

Before bed: stove and appliances off, space heaters and candles out, no dryer running overnight, doors locked and garage closed, bedroom doors shut, chargers off the bed, and a clear way out. Two minutes a night, and it adds up to a much safer house.
