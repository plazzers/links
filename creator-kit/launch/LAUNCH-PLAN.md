## 1. Intro and honesty rules

This plan is a set of ready-to-post copy for a small, honest launch. The approach: be useful first (free tools, guides, practical posts), mention the paid kit only where a community allows it, and let people decide for themselves.

**Honesty checklist: run through it before every post, email or DM**

- [ ] No fake reviews, star scores, user counts, download counts or "people are saying" quotes. If you don't have real, permissioned feedback, don't imply any.
- [ ] No income, view, subscriber or growth claims, and no screenshots of anyone's earnings.
- [ ] Never promise results, and avoid any wording that implies certain outcomes. The kit is a planning and production tool; results depend on the creator's content, niche and consistency.
- [ ] Disclose that you made it: "I built this" / "Disclosure: I made this tool."
- [ ] Read each community's rules (sidebar, pinned post, wiki) before posting. Many ban self-promotion, limit it to certain days or threads, or require a ratio of normal participation. Follow those rules as written; if in doubt, ask the moderators first.

- [ ] In communities, link to a free tool or a free guide, not the paid product, unless the rules clearly allow product posts.
- [ ] Describe features accurately (it does not create videos, voices or avatars and does not post to Pinterest for you).
- [ ] Every link carries the UTM tags for that channel.
- [ ] Reply to questions honestly, including "no, it doesn't do that."

### Reddit post 1: weekly workflow

**Suggested community type:** a general YouTube creators' subreddit or one focused on faceless / automation-style channels.

**Title:** The simple weekly workflow I use to plan a faceless channel without losing track of videos

**Body:**

I kept losing videos somewhere between "idea" and "uploaded," so I wrote down a weekly routine. Sharing in case it helps someone else running a faceless or AI-voice channel solo.

**The stages every video moves through:**
Idea → Script → Voice/Avatar → Edit → Thumbnail → Scheduled → Published

Writing the stages down sounds obvious, but having one board where every video sits in exactly one column is what stopped me from having three half-scripted videos and nothing ready to publish.

**A sample week (adjust to your own pace):**
- **Monday:** pick topics from a running list. Before committing, check it doesn't overlap with something you already made.
- **Tuesday:** outline and script. If you use an AI tool for drafts, give it your own points and structure first; edit the result in your own words.
- **Wednesday:** voice / avatar and rough edit.
- **Thursday:** finish the edit, write the description (hook, links, chapters, disclaimer).
- **Friday:** thumbnail and title. Write three title options and three thumbnail texts, then pick.
- **Weekend:** schedule, cut a couple of Shorts from your chapters, and do a 20-minute review of last week's numbers.

**Small things that helped:**
- Keep a buffer of one finished video if you can. It takes the pressure off a bad week.
- Thumbnail text should not repeat the title; 2–5 words is plenty.
- YouTube chapters need the first one at 0:00, at least three chapters, and each at least 10 seconds long.
- Read YouTube's policies on reused and repetitive content if you work with stock footage or AI voices.

What does your week look like? Curious how others split it up.

### Reddit post 2: honest title formulas

**Suggested community type:** a YouTube creators' subreddit, or one about thumbnails, titles and packaging.

**Title:** Title formulas that aren't clickbait, plus a quick checklist I run before publishing

**Body:**

A lot of title advice boils down to "make it shocking." I wanted formulas that are clear about what the video actually delivers. Here are the ones I keep coming back to:

1. **[Number] [things] [audience] should know about [topic]**: "7 Things New Gardeners Should Know About Clay Soil"
2. **How to [result] without [common pain]**: "How to Meal Prep Without Eating the Same Thing All Week"
3. **[Topic] explained in [time]**: "Index Funds Explained in 8 Minutes"
4. **Why [surprising but true thing]**: "Why Old Phones Slow Down (It's Not Planned)"
5. **[A] vs [B]: which one for [situation]?**: "Kindle vs Paperback: Which One for Travel?"
6. **The [adjective] guide to [topic] for [audience]**: "The Lazy Guide to Houseplants for Busy People"
7. **What happens when [scenario]**: "What Happens When You Stop Using Your Credit Card"
8. **[Mistakes] to avoid when [activity]**: "5 Mistakes to Avoid When Starting a Budget"
9. **I tried [thing] for [time]: here's what changed**: only if you actually did it.
10. **[Question your viewer types into search]?**: "Do You Need a Microphone for YouTube?"

**My pre-publish checklist:**
- Is the main keyword near the start?
- Roughly 40–70 characters, so it doesn't get cut off on mobile?
- Does it promise only what the video delivers?
- Does the thumbnail text add something instead of repeating the title?
- Would I click it if I didn't make it?

Which formulas work for your niche? I'm sure I'm missing some.

### Reddit post 3: Pinterest bulk CSV errors

**Suggested community type:** a Pinterest marketing subreddit, or a blogging / small-business marketing subreddit.

**Title:** Pinterest bulk upload CSV keeps failing? The errors I ran into and how I fixed each one

**Body:**

Pinterest lets you upload pins in bulk from a CSV file, which is great until the upload fails with a vague message. Here are the problems I hit and the fixes:

- **Wrong header line.** The column names need to match Pinterest's template exactly, including spelling and capitals. Download the current template from Pinterest's bulk-create screen and copy its header rather than typing your own.
- **Image links that aren't public.** Pinterest has to fetch each image from a public URL. Links to Google Drive or Dropbox "share" pages usually point at a web page, not the image file itself. The URL should end in the image (e.g. `.png` / `.jpg`) and open the bare image in a private browser window.
- **Saved in the wrong format.** Excel sometimes saves with a different encoding or separator. Save as plain CSV (UTF-8, comma as the delimiter).
- **Board names that don't match.** The board column has to match an existing board name exactly.
- **Titles and descriptions too long.** Keep titles and descriptions within Pinterest's current limits (check the template notes; they can change).
- **Publish dates in the past or in the wrong format.** Use the date format shown in Pinterest's template, and dates in the future.
- **Quotes and commas inside descriptions.** If a description contains commas, the field must be wrapped in quotes; stray quote marks break rows.

The fix that saved me the most time: check the file before uploading instead of after it fails.

Anyone found other gotchas? Happy to add them.

### Reddit post 4: free image hosting for Pinterest bulk upload

**Suggested community type:** a Pinterest marketing subreddit, or a subreddit for bloggers / solo creators.

**Title:** How I host pin images for free so Pinterest bulk upload can actually fetch them (GitHub Pages, no coding)

**Body:**

The bulk-upload CSV needs a public link to every image. If you don't have a website with a media library, here's a free way that works from the github.com website only:

1. Create a free GitHub account.
2. Create a new **public** repository (e.g. `pins`).
3. Click **Add file → Upload files** and drag in your pin images. Keep file names simple: lowercase, no spaces (`morning-routine-1.png`).
4. Go to **Settings → Pages**, set the source to your main branch, and save.
5. After a few minutes your images are live at `https://<your-username>.github.io/pins/<file-name>.png`.
6. Open one of those links in a private window to confirm it shows the bare image.
7. Put those links in the image column of your CSV.

**Notes:**
- Everything in a public repository is public, so only upload images you're happy to share.
- If you rename a file, its link changes, so update the CSV too.
- Compress images before uploading; pins load faster and the repository stays small.
- GitHub's menu names change from time to time; the steps are the same idea even if a label moves.

Questions welcome. I've broken this in most of the possible ways.

### Reddit post 5: weekly 20-minute review

**Suggested community type:** a YouTube creators' subreddit or a general content-creation / productivity subreddit.

**Title:** A 20-minute weekly review for YouTube + Pinterest that doesn't turn into staring at analytics all day

**Body:**

I used to open YouTube Studio daily and learn nothing. Now I do one short review a week and change one thing. The routine:

**1. Note the same few numbers every week (5 min)**
From YouTube Studio, for the videos published that week and the channel overall:
- impressions and click-through rate
- views and average view duration
- where views came from (search, suggested, browse, external)

From Pinterest analytics:
- impressions, outbound clicks, saves

Write them in the same place every week (a spreadsheet is fine).

**2. Compare with your own past, not other channels (5 min)**
Is this week's video above or below your usual? Look at your own last few videos, not someone else's channel.

**3. Ask one question (5 min)**
Low click-through → title / thumbnail. Low view duration → hook or pacing. Lots of search traffic → more on that topic.

**4. Pick one change for next week (5 min)**
Just one, so you can tell whether it made a difference. Write it down next to the numbers.

That's it. Small numbers bounce around a lot week to week, so look for patterns over a month or two before drawing conclusions.

What do you track?

## 9. Affiliate pitch

### Setting up affiliates on Payhip

Payhip has a built-in affiliate program: you set a commission percentage, affiliates sign up through your store, and Payhip gives each one their own link and tracks referred sales. Look for **Affiliates** in the dashboard (often under Marketing); as with coupons, menu names may change. Write a short affiliate terms note that includes the disclosure requirement below.

**Suggested commission: 30–40%.**

- **30%**: you keep more per sale, and it's still a reasonable offer for a low-priced product.
- **40%**: more attractive to creators deciding whether a low-priced product is worth mentioning, since the amount per sale on a ~$29 product is small either way. You keep less per sale.
- **Tradeoff:** a higher rate may make more creators willing to mention it; a lower rate leaves more for you on each sale. Also consider whether the commission applies to discounted (LAUNCH30) sales and say so clearly. A reasonable start is 35%, adjusted after a month if needed. Don't change the rate on existing affiliates without telling them first.

### Disclosure requirement (include in your terms and every pitch)

Affiliates must clearly disclose the affiliate relationship wherever they share the link, in line with FTC (US), ASA/CAP (UK) and similar rules: a plain statement such as "I get a commission if you buy through this link" placed near the link, not hidden in a link tree or at the end of a long description. If they received free access, they should say that too. They should not describe results, income or views they haven't had, and should not promise results to their audience.

## 10. Tracking

Once a week (Sunday works; it fits the weekly review), write down the following in one spreadsheet or note. No targets, just a record, so you can compare week to week.

**Visits and sources**
- Payhip product page visits, and the referrers / sources Payhip shows. Note which `utm_source` values appear (reddit, facebook, discord, producthunt, x, threads, email, affiliate).
- If the site has any analytics, note visits to the creator-kit pages, the two free tools and the guides, by source.

**Sales**
- Number of sales, and how many used `LAUNCH30`.
- Sales attributed to affiliates (Payhip's affiliate report).
- Refund requests, and the reason given.

**Sign-ups**
- New free-tool / FREE_URL sign-ups this week, and unsubscribes.
- Replies to emails (count, and the most common question).

**Pinterest**
- From Pinterest analytics: impressions, outbound clicks and saves for the kit pins.

**Community**
- Which posts got real questions or useful feedback (link to them).
- Any moderator feedback or removed posts, and what to change.

**One-line takeaway**
- One thing to keep and one thing to change next week.

Small numbers bounce around a lot; look at the pattern over the four weeks rather than any single week.
