How to Write a YouTube Description: Links, Chapters and Disclosures

A simple YouTube description template: a clear first two lines, the right links, chapters, hashtags and an honest disclosure, with a copy-ready layout.

Most viewers never expand a YouTube description. The few who do are often the most interested people you have: they want the link you mentioned, the source for a claim, or a way to jump to the part they care about. A good description serves them quickly and helps YouTube understand what the video is about. This guide gives you a layout you can reuse on every video, with notes on links, chapters, hashtags and disclosures.

It won't turn a description into a sales page, and it shouldn't. The aim is a description that is clear, honest and easy to act on.

The first two lines do most of the work

On the watch page, only the opening of the description shows before the viewer taps "more". In search results, YouTube may also show a short piece of it. So the first two lines should do two things:

  1. Say what the video covers, in plain words. Use the phrase a viewer would search for, once, naturally. "How to repot a snake plant without damaging the roots" beats "In today's video we're talking about plants!"
  2. Offer the one action that matters most. If you want viewers to grab a free checklist or read a related guide, put that link here, with a short reason to click.

Keep the first line readable on its own. A wall of hashtags, a list of social profiles or "Hey guys!" wastes the only part most people see.

A useful test: read your title, thumbnail text and first description line together. They should add to each other, not repeat the same words three times. The YouTube title formulas guide and the thumbnail text rules cover the other two pieces.

A description template you can copy

Here is a layout that works for most faceless and voiceover channels. Fill it in from your script after the video is finished, not before.

Line 1: What the video covers, with your main search phrase.
Line 2: Your main link + one short reason to click.

A short paragraph (2–4 sentences) with more detail:
who it's for, what they'll be able to do, anything to know first.

Chapters
0:00 Intro
0:42 First chapter title
2:15 Second chapter title
...

Links mentioned
- Free checklist: <link>
- Related video: <link>
- Sources: <link>

Disclosure (if any): which links are affiliate links,
or that the video includes a paid promotion.

#hashtag1 #hashtag2 #hashtag3

You don't need every block on every video. A short tutorial might only need lines one and two, chapters and a sources link. What matters is that the order stays the same, so returning viewers know where to look.

Links: fewer, clearer, and tracked

Descriptions can hold many links, but a long list makes each one less likely to be clicked. A good habit is one main link near the top and a short "Links mentioned" list further down.

  • Label every link. "Free planning checklist:" tells people what they get. A bare URL doesn't.
  • Link to what you said in the video. If you mention a guide, a product or a source, the matching link should be easy to find.
  • Use full, working links. Test each one in a private browser window before publishing. Broken links in old videos are common and easy to miss.
  • Tag your own links. Adding UTM tags to links that go to your own site or store lets you see which video sent the visitor. The UTM links for YouTubers guide explains the format and a simple naming system.

One thing to know for Shorts: links in Shorts descriptions and comments are not clickable. For Shorts, use the related video feature to point viewers to a long video, and put the link in that long video's description instead.

Chapters belong in the description

Chapters are created from timestamps in the description. The basic rules are: the first timestamp must be 0:00, you need at least three timestamps in ascending order, and each chapter must be at least 10 seconds long. YouTube's Video Chapters help page has the current details, and our guide to YouTube chapters and timestamps covers how to write good chapter titles and fix chapters that don't show.

Put the chapter list after your short paragraph and before the links. Viewers looking for a specific part find it quickly, and the list doubles as a quick summary of the video.

Hashtags: a few, relevant ones

Hashtags in the description can make a video appear on hashtag pages, and YouTube may show up to three of them near the title. Keep them few and relevant:

  • Use two to five hashtags that describe the topic or series, not trending tags that have nothing to do with the video.
  • Avoid stuffing. YouTube's guidelines say that if a video has more than 60 hashtags, all of its hashtags are ignored, and excessive or misleading tags can lead to the video being removed from search.
  • Put them at the end of the description so they don't crowd the first lines.

Disclosures: say it plainly

If a link earns you a commission, or the video includes a paid promotion, say so in plain words near the link. For example: "Some links below are affiliate links; if you buy through them I may earn a commission at no extra cost to you."

YouTube also has its own setting: if a video includes paid product placement, sponsorship or endorsement, tick the paid promotion box in the video's details in YouTube Studio. Viewers then see a short notice on the video. The rules on disclosing paid relationships also depend on where you and your viewers are, so check the official guidance for your country. This guide isn't legal advice.

If you use realistic AI-generated or altered content, there is a separate disclosure setting. The AI voice and avatar channels guide explains when YouTube asks for it.

Write it once, reuse the structure

You don't need to write every description from scratch. Save the template above in your notes app and fill it in for each video:

  1. After the edit is locked, copy your chapter timestamps from the editing timeline.
  2. Write lines one and two from the video's one-sentence promise, the same sentence you used to write the title.
  3. Add the short paragraph from your script's intro.
  4. Paste your links and check each one.
  5. Add the disclosure if anything needs one.
  6. Read it on your phone before publishing. If the opening lines look cluttered on a small screen, trim them.

Keep a few reusable blocks too: a standard "about this channel" sentence, your sources format and your disclosure wording. Then each description takes a few minutes, not half an hour.

A short checklist before you publish

  • The first line says what the video covers, in plain words
  • The main link is in the first two lines, with a reason to click
  • Every link is labeled and tested
  • Chapters start at 0:00, with at least three, each 10 seconds or longer
  • Two to five relevant hashtags at the end
  • Any affiliate links or paid promotions are clearly disclosed
  • Nothing in the description promises more than the video delivers

If your titles still need work, run your drafts through the free YouTube Title Scorer and see how each one does on length, a strong start and your keyword. Then let the description carry the details the title can't.

Over time, look at which links actually get clicks. If one link is never used, cut it. A shorter description that people act on is better than a long one they skip. The weekly content review shows how to fit that check into a 20-minute routine.
