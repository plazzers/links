How to Plan a Faceless YouTube Channel: A Simple Weekly Workflow

A day-by-day weekly workflow for planning a faceless YouTube channel: topics, scripts, voice, edit, thumbnails, Shorts and a look at YouTube's rules.

A faceless channel is still a channel: viewers come back for a clear topic, a recognizable format and videos that actually teach or entertain them. The hard part is rarely the editing. It is keeping a steady rhythm when you are the writer, narrator, editor and marketer at once. This guide gives you a plain weekly workflow you can run alone, plus the platform rules you need to respect so your work stays eligible for monetization.

Decide what the channel is before you plan anything

Planning only helps once you know what you are planning for. Write these down in a single note and keep it open while you work:

  1. Topic in one sentence. For example: "Short explainers about how everyday money products actually work."
  2. Who it is for. One kind of viewer, described plainly ("people setting up their first budget").
  3. Your format. Pick one main format to start: a listicle explainer, a story-style reveal or a how-to. You can add more later.
  4. Your "faceless" method. Stock footage, screen recordings, animation, slides, an AI voice, an AI avatar or your own voice. Decide once so you are not re-deciding every week.
  5. What you add. Your research, your opinion, your examples, your structure. This line matters more than it looks; see the policy section below.

Then fill a topics list with 20 to 30 ideas. Do not polish them yet. You only need enough that you never start a week with a blank page.

Know YouTube's rules on AI and repetitive content

Two policies shape how faceless channels should be planned. Check YouTube's help pages for the current wording, because details change.

  • Altered or synthetic content disclosure. If a video contains realistic content that was made or meaningfully changed with AI or other tools, and a viewer could mistake it for real people, places or events, YouTube asks you to disclose it in YouTube Studio when you upload. An AI voice reading your own script over charts is generally not the concern; a realistic AI person saying things a real person never said is. When unsure, read the current guidance and disclose.
  • Mass-produced or repetitive content. The YouTube Partner Program does not monetize content that is mass-produced, repetitive or made from templates with little variation (often described as "inauthentic content"). A channel that uploads near-identical videos with swapped keywords is at risk, faceless or not.

The practical answer is the same for both: plan for real value in every video. Original research, a clear point of view, useful examples and honest labels. Build that into your workflow rather than hoping to add it at the end.

The weekly workflow, day by day

This schedule assumes one long video and two or three Shorts per week. Shift days to fit your life; the order matters more than the dates.

Day Main task Done when
Monday Pick the topic and outline Outline with 4 to 7 points
Tuesday Write and edit the script Script read aloud once
Wednesday Voice or avatar, gather visuals Audio file and asset folder ready
Thursday Edit the video Full rough cut exported
Friday Titles, thumbnail, description, chapters Upload scheduled
Saturday Shorts and Pinterest pins Shorts scheduled, pins queued
Sunday Weekly review, rest Numbers noted, next topic chosen

Monday: topic and outline

Pick one idea from your topics list. Check it does not repeat a video you already made. Write an outline: a hook, the main points in order and an ending that tells the viewer what to do next.

Tuesday: script

Draft the script, by hand or with an AI tool. If you use AI, give it your outline, your examples and your tone, then rewrite the result in your own words. Our guide on AI script prompts that sound human covers how to brief the tool so the draft needs less fixing. Read the final script aloud once; stumbles on the page become stumbles in the audio.

Wednesday: voice and visuals

Record your voice or generate the AI voice or avatar in the tool you use. Collect footage, screenshots and graphics into one folder named after the video. Note anything that needs a synthetic-content disclosure.

Thursday: edit

Edit to the script. Aim for a finished rough cut, not perfection. Watch it once at normal speed and fix only what would confuse a viewer.

Friday: packaging and upload

Write three title options and two or three thumbnail text options, then choose. Keep thumbnail text to 2 to 5 words and readable on a phone; see our thumbnail text rules. Run your titles through the free YouTube Title Scorer to catch length and clarity issues. Write the description and add chapters: the first timestamp must be 0:00, you need at least three chapters, and each must be at least 10 seconds long. Schedule the upload and tick the disclosure box if it applies.

Saturday: Shorts and promotion

Turn two or three chapters into vertical Shorts (they can be up to 3 minutes at the time of writing). Each Short needs its own hook and on-screen text. If you use Pinterest, make pins for the new video. Our guide on turning chapters into Shorts walks through this step.

Sunday: review

Spend 20 minutes noting your numbers and choosing next week's topic. Keep it short and look for one change to try.

Track every video on one board

When you run the whole pipeline alone, it is easy to lose track of which video is waiting on a voiceover and which is ready to schedule. A simple board with columns fixes that:

  • Idea
  • Script
  • Voice/Avatar
  • Edit
  • Thumbnail
  • Scheduled
  • Published

Move each video card one column at a time. A spreadsheet, a notes app or a paper list all work. The Faceless Creator Kit's Channel Planner uses exactly these columns and adds a calendar and a weekly review, if you would rather not build it yourself.

Keep a buffer, not a backlog

A one-video buffer (one finished video scheduled ahead) protects you from a bad week. A pile of twenty half-finished ideas does the opposite. Some ways to keep a healthy buffer:

  1. Batch similar tasks when you have extra time, such as writing two scripts on one Tuesday.
  2. Keep your topics list long but your "in progress" column short, ideally two or three cards.
  3. Drop ideas that have sat in "Idea" for months without pulling you back.

A short checklist before you publish

Run this list every Friday:

  • The title says what the video is about and is well under 100 characters.
  • The thumbnail text is 2 to 5 words and matches the title's promise without repeating it word for word.
  • The thumbnail image is 1280×720.
  • Chapters start at 0:00, there are at least three, each at least 10 seconds.
  • The description explains the video in its first two lines.
  • Realistic synthetic content is disclosed in YouTube Studio.
  • The video adds something of your own: research, opinion, examples or structure.

Start small and adjust weekly

You do not need a perfect system on week one. Run this workflow for four weeks, then look at what kept slipping. If editing always spills into Saturday, give it two days and make fewer Shorts. If ideas run dry, spend one Monday only on topics. The plan exists to serve the videos, and there is no promise of views from any schedule; consistency and genuinely useful content are simply what you can control.
