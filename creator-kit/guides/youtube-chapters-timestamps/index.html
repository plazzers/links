YouTube Chapters and Timestamps: Rules, Titles and Fixes

How YouTube chapters work: the timestamp rules, how to write useful chapter titles, automatic chapters, and what to check when chapters don't show.

Chapters split a YouTube video into labeled sections on the progress bar. Viewers can see what's coming, skip to the part they need and come back to a specific step later. For tutorials, explainers and list videos, which covers a lot of faceless channels, chapters make a long video much easier to use. This guide covers the rules YouTube checks, how to write chapter titles people understand, and how to fix chapters that don't appear.

YouTube's own Video Chapters help page is the source for the current rules. Check it if anything below looks different in your account.

The rules YouTube checks

You add chapters by typing a list of timestamps and titles in the video description. For YouTube to turn that list into chapters, it has to follow a few rules:

  • The first timestamp is 0:00. Not 0:01, not 0:05. If your intro is short, the first chapter can simply be "Intro".
  • At least three timestamps. Two sections won't create chapters.
  • Ascending order. Each timestamp must come later than the one before it.
  • Each chapter is at least 10 seconds long. Very short sections, such as a quick title card, should be merged into the chapter next to them.

A correct list looks like this:

0:00 Intro
0:38 What you need
1:52 Step 1: Clean the filter
4:10 Step 2: Check the seal
6:25 Common mistakes
8:02 Quick recap

Each line starts with the timestamp, then a space, then the title. For videos over an hour, use the hour format, such as 1:05:30.

Writing chapter titles people understand

Chapter titles show on the progress bar and in the chapter list, often cut short on phones. They work best when each one names a single idea in plain words.

  • Keep them short. Around two to six words is easy to read on a small screen.
  • Lead with the useful word. "Step 2: Check the seal" tells viewers more than "Now let's move on".
  • Match what's on screen. If the chapter is called "Common mistakes", the section should actually be about mistakes.
  • Avoid teasers. Chapter titles are navigation, not clickbait. "You won't believe this part" helps nobody find anything.
  • Number steps in tutorials. "Step 1", "Step 2" helps viewers follow along and come back to where they stopped.

If you plan your script in sections, your chapter titles are almost written already. One chapter per script section is a good default. The faceless channel planning guide shows how a section-by-section script fits into a weekly workflow.

Automatic chapters vs your own

YouTube can also generate chapters automatically for some videos. In YouTube Studio, the video's details include a setting to allow automatic chapters. If you add your own timestamps in the description, YouTube uses yours instead.

Automatic chapters can be a reasonable fallback, but writing your own has clear advantages:

  • You choose the break points, so each chapter starts at a natural place in the script.
  • You choose the wording, so titles match your topic and your viewers' words.
  • You can reuse the same list for your Shorts plan, your pins and your description.

If you don't want automatic chapters on a video, you can turn the setting off in Studio.

Chapters not showing? Check these first

When a chapter list doesn't turn into chapters, the cause is usually one of these:

  1. The first timestamp isn't 0:00. This is the most common mistake. "00:00" also works; "0:01" does not.
  2. Fewer than three timestamps. Add a section or split a long one.
  3. A chapter shorter than 10 seconds. Look for two timestamps very close together and merge them.
  4. Timestamps out of order. Often caused by editing the video after writing the list. Re-copy the times from your final edit.
  5. Extra characters before the timestamp. A bullet, a dash or an emoji at the start of a line can stop the timestamp from being read. Put the time first.
  6. The video was just updated. Changes to the description can take a little time to show.
  7. Automatic chapters are set but nothing was generated. Automatic chapters aren't available for every video. Adding your own list solves this.

YouTube's help page also notes that chapters may not be available on some videos or channels. If your list follows every rule and still doesn't show, that page is the place to check.

Chapters do more than help viewers

Once you have a good chapter list, reuse it:

  • The description. The chapter list is a quick outline of the video, right where curious viewers look. The YouTube description guide shows where to place it.
  • Shorts. Each chapter is a candidate for a Short, since it already holds one idea. The chapters to Shorts guide walks through that, and how many Shorts to cut from one long video helps you decide how many to make.
  • Pins and posts. A chapter title like "3 signs your seal is worn" can become a pin headline that links back to the video.
  • Your weekly review. In YouTube Studio's retention graph, you can see roughly where viewers drop off. Chapter boundaries make it easier to say which section lost them.

A simple workflow for every video

  1. While scripting, give each section a short working title.
  2. After the edit is locked, note the start time of each section from your editing timeline.
  3. Write the list with 0:00 first, at least three entries and no section under 10 seconds.
  4. Tidy the titles so each is short, plain and specific.
  5. Paste the list into the description, below the opening lines.
  6. Check the published video on a phone: tap the progress bar and see that the chapters appear and the titles are readable.

Quick checklist

  • First timestamp is 0:00
  • At least three chapters, in ascending order
  • Every chapter 10 seconds or longer
  • Each line starts with the timestamp, no symbols before it
  • Short, plain titles that name one idea each
  • Times copied from the final edit, not the draft
